# Session 5: Hybrid Instruments and Asset Monetization

## The Story (Part One — Idle Assets)

A company owns an old warehouse it bought decades ago, sitting on the books at that ancient purchase price. Nobody's really using it much anymore — but its actual market value today is many times what it was originally worth. Instead of selling it and losing the space, the company sells it to an investor and immediately leases it back — same building, same operations, uninterrupted — except now there's a pile of unlocked cash sitting in the bank that was previously just... frozen inside four walls.

## The Story (Part Two — Delaying the Choice)

A growing but not-yet-fully-proven company needs funding, but isn't confident enough to fully commit either way. So it issues a convertible bond: for now, it's just debt — fixed interest, nothing dramatic. But if the company does spectacularly well later, the lender has the *right* to convert that debt into equity and share in the upside instead. If the company does just okay, the lender simply gets their money back as a normal loan.

## The Concept

Idle assets can be unlocked for cash without disrupting how the business runs (asset monetization), and hybrid instruments let both sides delay the hard debt-vs-equity decision until there's more clarity about how things turn out.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

Companies sitting on old real estate, unused equipment, or underleveraged assets are everywhere — airlines, retail chains, manufacturing firms, even PSUs. A sharp manager spots "we're sitting on value we're not using" long before anyone else does, and proposing a sale-leaseback can unlock serious cash without touching operations. Similarly, when a company's future is genuinely uncertain, pushing for a hybrid instrument instead of a rigid straight loan or straight equity deal often gets a better deal for everyone — this is common in growth-stage funding rounds and expansion financing.

## The One-Liner

*"Idle assets are quietly bleeding money — unlock what you're just sitting on, and when the future's unclear, let a hybrid deal share the uncertainty instead of forcing a choice too early."*

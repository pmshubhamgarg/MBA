# Session 17: Structural Default Models, CAMELS, and the Foundations of DCF Valuation

## The Story

A struggling supplier's official numbers look "okay" on paper. But when you actually estimate the real gap between what they own and what they owe, you realize they're much closer to collapse than their own reports admit. Separately, two analysts value the exact same company using the exact same valuation formula — and get wildly different "correct" answers, because they quietly assumed different growth rates and risk levels.

## The Concept

Structural default models estimate a company's *real* distance to failure, beyond the surface numbers. And any DCF valuation is only as good as its weakest assumption — the formula is honest, but the inputs are opinions.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

Whenever someone hands you a valuation, a forecast, or an assessment of a partner's financial health, your job isn't to accept the number — it's to find the assumption hiding underneath it. This single habit separates people who get talked into bad deals from people who don't.

## The One-Liner

*"Distrust the headline number until you've found the assumption hiding behind it."*

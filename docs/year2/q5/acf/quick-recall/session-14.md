# Session 14: International Financial Management — Currency Exposure and Parity Conditions

## The Story

A company's overseas subsidiary reports a loss for the quarter. Panic sets in — until someone points out: sales were exactly on target, every invoice got collected on time, nothing operationally went wrong. The "loss" only exists because the rupee moved against the local currency between two reporting dates. Nobody made a mistake. The exchange rate just moved.

## The Concept

This is translation exposure — one of three distinct types of currency risk (alongside transaction exposure, from actual foreign payments, and economic exposure, from bigger shifts like tariffs). Currencies and interest rates move together across borders for real, explainable reasons (interest rate parity, purchasing power parity) — it's not random.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

If you ever manage or report on anything international, you will eventually have to explain a "bad number" that has nothing to do with anyone's actual performance. Knowing the difference between a real operational failure and a currency-driven paper loss protects your team from being unfairly blamed — and protects you from panicking over nothing.

## The One-Liner

*"Not every loss on paper is a failure on the ground — sometimes it's just the currency talking."*

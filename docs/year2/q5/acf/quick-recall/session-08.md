# Session 8: Share Buybacks and Introduction to Risk Management

## The Story

Your company had an amazing year and wants to reward the team. Option one: give everyone a bonus — one-time, everyone's thrilled, and next year if things are tighter, nobody expects it again. Option two: give everyone a permanent raise — also great, but now it's baked into next year's budget forever, whether or not the good year repeats.

Separately, that same quarter, your company signs its first client contract priced in US dollars. Nobody thinks much of it — until six months later someone realizes the rupee has moved and the deal is now worth noticeably less than expected, for reasons that have nothing to do with how well anyone actually did their job.

## The Concept

A buyback is like the bonus — flexible, one-time, no future obligation. Raising the dividend is like the raise — a promise you're now expected to keep forever. And the moment any part of your business touches a foreign currency, you've inherited a real risk that exists whether or not you were thinking about it — this is where risk management stops being theoretical.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

When a company has surplus cash, deciding between a buyback and a bigger dividend is exactly the bonus-vs-raise decision — and picking wrong creates either wasted goodwill or a commitment you can't sustain. And the currency lesson matters far beyond finance teams: any manager whose business touches an international client, vendor, or market has inherited exposure the moment that deal was signed, whether or not anyone flagged it.

## The One-Liner

*"A buyback is a bonus, a dividend increase is a raise — know which one you can actually take back."*

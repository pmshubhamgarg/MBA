# Session 16: Financial Distress, IBC Resolution, and Predicting Default

## The Story

A business partner starts paying vendors a little late, tightening terms with suppliers, going quiet on calls. None of it screams "collapse" on its own — but taken together, it's an early warning pattern. A manager who notices this early can renegotiate terms, add protections, or plan ahead. One who ignores it gets blindsided months later when the actual failure hits.

## The Concept

Financial distress rarely arrives suddenly — there are early warning signals (delayed payments, tightening credit, unusual patterns) long before real failure. And there's an actual formal legal process (insolvency resolution) built to handle failure in an orderly way — understanding it removes fear and turns panic into a plan.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

Watching for early distress signals in a vendor, client, or partner — instead of being surprised later — is one of the most practically valuable habits from this entire course. It lets you negotiate protective clauses *before* trouble hits, not scramble after.

## The One-Liner

*"Distress never arrives suddenly — it always leaves warning signs first, and the ones who spot them early get to negotiate, not panic."*

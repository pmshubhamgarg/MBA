### The Modash Demo (Influencer Platforms, Quickly)

Modash offers two ways to find creators: an **AI natural-language search** ("creator for young professionals balancing work and personal growth") and a **filter search** (follower count, growth rate, location, engagement rate, gender, age, language, and a neat **fake-follower filter** to screen out bots).

> **Professor:** "This natural language prompt... isn't the greatest. You might be better off using the filters as opposed to the natural language prompt. Plus, you need to go through a round of manual validation."

The platform's real value is twofold: **basic due-diligence** on each creator, and managing the full **"code-to-cash" lifecycle** — discover, engage, agree commercials, money sits in **escrow** until milestones are delivered, then it releases. It is premium (credits or subscription), but free trials need no card.

## The Big Frame: Three Jobs AI Does in Go-To-Market

Before any tool, the professor set the mental model. AI shows up in marketing and sales in **three broad buckets**:

| Bucket | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Generative AI** | Creates content | Social assets, LinkedIn posts, long-form blogs |
| **2. Decision Augmentation** | A thought-partner for better decisions | A trained AI agent that critiques your GTM deck |
| **3. Workflow Automation** | Executes repetitive flows | Social scheduling, B2B prospecting outreach |

### Decision Augmentation — Don't Hand It a Blank Slate

The professor was emphatic about *how* to use an LLM for thinking. Treat it like the smartest generalist alive who knows nothing about **your** business ("your *danda*").

> **Professor:** "Don't go to the AI engine and say 'Give me this, help me create a strategy.' That is always a bad idea. Develop your own initial point of view, then share that and ask it to point out mistakes."

The trick: **anchor it on a persona.** Preparing a CEO deck for a Malaysia entry? Build the deck yourself, then ask the LLM to play a **hard-nosed, critical CEO** (feed it the real CEO's LinkedIn profile), and have it attack your logic and preempt questions.

> **Professor:** "Almost every other deck today is made using Claude slides. Nobody's even reading what's in the deck. That is a bad idea. Do the grunt work yourself; ask the AI to make it better and point out mistakes — after giving it a role. If you don't give it a role, it'll hallucinate and start rambling."

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
**The three AI buckets in GTM: Generative AI (content), Decision Augmentation (thought-partner), Workflow Automation (execution).** And the golden rule of decision augmentation — **bring your own POV first, then make the AI critique it in a defined role/persona.** Never start from a blank slate.
:::

### Agentic Automation vs Plain Automation

People use "AI agent" and "automation" interchangeably. They are not the same.

| Plain Workflow Automation | Agentic Automation |
|---|---|
| Follows fixed rules — "fairly dumb" | Has memory and context |
| No judgement | Has **decision-making and discretionary power** |
| E.g. auto-schedule a post | E.g. research a prospect, decide the angle, personalise outreach |

> **Professor:** "All agentic workflows are automated, but the converse is not true."

:::tip[Memory Hook — "Create, Decide, Automate"]
The three AI jobs in marketing. Everything else in this session is a tool serving one of these three.
:::

## SEO Story 1: Thrillophilia — Flanking the Giants

**Thrillophilia** (holidays and experiences) survived a cut-throat travel-tech market against Booking.com, MakeMyTrip, OYO, Cleartrip and more — while **100% bootstrapped**, on founders' own money, no VC.

> **Professor:** "The only thing they did was SEO. They had no money to do anything else. Their marketing budget is a big fat zero even today."

Most founders make the same mistake: they chase the **highest-search-volume keyword** ("resorts in Manali"), which is exactly where entrenched giants already rank page one. For a new entrant it is next to impossible.

> **Professor:** "If you're not on page one, it's as good as not being there. Nobody goes to page two."

Thrillophilia's **flanking strategy**: ignore the head terms, go after **hundreds of long-tail keywords** with **low search volume but also low ranking difficulty** — "holidays and experiences in Mount Abu," "in Kotagiri," tiny hill stations most people never heard of. Each one small, but stacked together they delivered quick page-one wins and compounding growth.

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
**The flanking strategy:** new entrants can't win high-volume/high-difficulty head keywords. Target **many long-tail keywords where search volume is low AND keyword difficulty is low.** Keyword volume and keyword difficulty usually move together — so the smart play is the low-low corner.
:::

### SEMrush — Reading Keyword Difficulty

The professor demoed **SEMrush** (his favourite keyword-analytics tool). Entering "online payments" surfaces two numbers that matter:

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Search Volume** | Monthly searches (e.g. 74,000 in India) |
| **Keyword Difficulty (KD)** | 0–100 score — how hard it is to rank on page one |

The novice error is to pick a keyword purely on high volume. Even **Neil Patel** says any SEO project takes a **minimum six months** to show results — so chasing impossible head terms just leads to burnout and "SEO doesn't work for me." Note too that Google's **AI Overview** now sits on top of results, skimming off clicks before users scroll.

:::note[Good to Know]
Other keyword-analytics tools the professor recommends: **Moz, Ahrefs, Ubersuggest** — alongside SEMrush.
:::

## SEO Story 2: Practo and Domain Authority

**Practto (Practo)**, India's largest online doctor marketplace, gets roughly **30 million visits a month**, and **~90% comes from SEO** — essentially free traffic (no CPC, only time and effort to rank).

Their SEO is so strong that if a gynaecologist in Gurgaon has their own website *and* a Practo listing, **Practo's listing outranks the doctor's own site** — so patients book via Practo, and the doctor shares a fee.

The engine behind this is **Domain Authority (DA)** — a 0–100 score for how authoritative Google considers your whole domain. Higher DA means every page under that root domain has a better shot at ranking.

| Domain | Approx. DA |
|---|---|
| google.com, youtube.com | 90+ (platforms) |
| **Wikipedia.org** | 90+ |
| Razorpay.com | ~70 (industry leader) |
| Zerodha | ~75 |

> **Professor:** "Getting 90-plus is next to impossible."

### The Wikipedia Backlink Trick

A quick DA booster: get a **backlink from Wikipedia** (a 90-plus authority source). At HealthifyMe, the team hunted Wikipedia pages with **broken citation links** (leading to 404s) — say a healthy high-protein vegan smoothie recipe — created their own replacement page, and pitched the Wikipedia editors to use it as the citation. Instant authoritative backlink.

:::tip[Important]
**Domain Authority (0–100)** is the compounding asset of SEO. Every blog, product page and backlink you earn snowballs into DA, which lifts *all* your pages. Backlinks from high-DA sources (Wikipedia, major publications) are worth far more than many low-quality ones.
:::

## The Content Toolchain — and the AI Content Trap

You *can* generate SEO blogs with ChatGPT or Claude, but they are generalists. Specialists exist: **Rightsonic** and **Jasper** for SEO-optimised content. The professor demoed **Leafpad**, a free AI blog generator — feed it a URL (he used Razorpay), it studies the site and ICP, and drafts a long-form article (e.g. on payroll compliance) in 5–10 minutes.

But **do not copy-paste it straight to your blog.** Two reasons:

**1. The plagiarism myth — and why it's not the real problem.** It is *not* plagiarism (generative AI creates net-new content by reasoning across ~200 sources, like a research assistant who reads 200 places instead of 20). The real problem is **it lacks deep company context** — it is a large language model, not a small one trained on your business. Treat it as a **junior writer's first draft** that still needs editing.

**2. Google is penalising AI content at scale.** Sites that spun up hundreds/thousands of AI product and blog pages overnight saw impression spikes — and Google has started **de-listing and blacklisting** them for these dark patterns.

> **Professor:** "Treat AI still as a very good assistant. There needs to be a human-in-the-loop review system before you publish."

:::tip[Important]
**Human-in-the-loop is mandatory.** AI content = fast first draft, never the final publish. Going overboard (mass AI pages) now actively *hurts* your SEO.
:::

### SimilarWeb — Competitive Intelligence in a Click

**SimilarWeb** is a browser plug-in (and paid tool) that reads traffic patterns for **any URL, any page** — not just your own. For Amazon.in it showed ~half a billion visits/month, ~40% bounce rate, ~7 pages/visit, ~3 min sessions, plus the **traffic-source mix**:

| Source | Amazon.in share | What it signals |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | ~40% | People type the URL directly — **powerful brand** |
| **Organic / SEO** | ~30% | SEO is far from dead — ~150M visits/month |
| **Paid / SEM** | ~10% | ~50M paid visits/month |
| Referral / Social | remainder | — |

High **direct traffic** is a brand-strength proxy. SimilarWeb also now shows **AI chat traffic distribution** (how much comes via ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) and top prompts — gold for competitors.

:::note[Good to Know]
For **app** analytics (not browser), the equivalent paid tool is **App Annie**.
:::

## GEO: Winning the Large Language Model

**GEO — Generative Engine Optimization** — is the new discipline (barely a year old): making sure **AI LLMs recommend and cite your brand**. Ask an LLM "best payment gateway in India?" and you want it to say *Razorpay*. (ChatGPT has even begun piloting ads in India — with Razorpay facilitating.)

So what drives GEO? Here is the counter-intuitive part. The professor framed it as: *if I asked you "which is India's number one payment gateway," would you trust each company's own website claiming it's number one?*

> **Professor:** "You'd give very little credit to the website content. You need a website as a digital anchor, but the content per se doesn't matter as much in the eyes of the LLM."

What the LLM *does* weigh:

- **Brand mentions / earned media** — citations across YouTube, Reddit (now being clamped down after spam abuse), LinkedIn
- **Reviews and ratings** — G2, TrustRadius, HighRadius for B2B; app-store ratings; social sentiment scores for B2C
- **Analyst reports** — being featured in a **Gartner** or **Forrester**
- **Authoritative long-form content** — YouTube, thought leadership

> **Professor:** "GEO, in my view, is nothing but a new-fangled term for brand marketing. How strong is your brand coming through in earned-media channels?"

:::tip[Memory Hook — "GEO = Brand Marketing in disguise"]
SEO rewards **backlinks**; GEO rewards **brand mentions and earned media**. If your brand is cited everywhere credible, the LLM cites you too.
:::

### GEO Story: Simon-Kucher — Invisible on Google, #1 on the LLM

**Simon-Kucher**, a 40-year-old German firm (~700 million dollars revenue), is the world's #1 consultancy for **pricing and packaging** — yet almost unknown in India. Ask Perplexity, Claude, Gemini or ChatGPT "world's number one pricing and packaging firm?" and they almost unanimously answer **Simon-Kucher** — not BCG, McKinsey or Bain.

The irony: on that same Gemini results page, Simon-Kucher's **AI Overview names them #1**, but scroll down into the blue-link **SEO** results and they appear **nowhere on page one**. They did a "bad job" of SEO — and it doesn't matter, because their GEO is strong: analyst reports, management reviews, LinkedIn thought leadership, strong G2/TrustRadius reviews, a defensible niche.

### GEO vs SEO — Which Matters When?

| You sell to… | Prioritise | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Large enterprises / senior CXOs** (high value, ~million-dollar deals) | **GEO** | A CRO won't pick a vendor off a Google result; they **validate** choices via LLMs |
| **B2C / SMBs / startups** (high volume, value game) | **Both — SEO still vital** | Discovery and traffic are a numbers game |

The professor's proof that **SEO is not dead**:

- **OpenAI posted a job for an SEO Strategist.** "If Sam Altman wants an SEO strategist, you know SEO as a job function will continue to thrive."
- **Google handles ~5 trillion searches/year** (first time officially disclosed, early 2025). **ChatGPT gets ~70–80 billion prompts/month** — roughly **12% of Google's volume** — and *both* are still growing fast (Google search ad revenue growing ~25% QoQ on a ~50-billion-dollar base).

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
**SEO vs GEO is situational, not either/or.** Selling to **senior enterprise decision-makers** → GEO wins (validation, not discovery). Selling **B2C / SMB / high-volume** → SEO remains essential and GEO complements it. SEO is far from dead — OpenAI itself hired an SEO strategist.
:::

## The Group Exercise: A US Go-To-Market for B2B SaaS

The class split into breakout groups as the **CMO of a B2B SaaS company entering the US, targeting large enterprises**, designing a GTM across four points: **channel mix, influencer strategy, keyword/content strategy, AI tools.** (Teams converged on LinkedIn + ABM, Gartner/Forrester relationships, intent-based keywords, case studies, GEO-first.) The professor's recurring recommendations are the exam gold:

**1. Founder personal branding is the #1 channel.**
> **Professor:** "People trust people more than they trust a company. The founder is the biggest influencer for your business. I would not lead with ads — I'd lead with content and thought leadership."

**2. Work with "rain-makers" / GTM advisors.** Senior industry veterans with strong networks and **no conflict of interest** make qualified introductions — paid **100% variable**, a cut of the deal (e.g. ~5% of a million-dollar sale). This is exactly the professor's own role at Simon-Kucher entering India.

**3. Brand building *before* demand gen.**
> **Professor:** "Prioritise brand building first in a new market before demand gen. When you're an unknown entity, generating leads is very hard. Demand gen should be a secondary objective in the early days."

**4. Position as global, not Indian.** For B2B — especially fintech, where trust is everything — the "cultural stigma" of being an Indian company matters. Use PR in big publications and an **American face** for the US business.

**5. Zoom out to a bigger theme, and defend retention.** Razorpay built content not around "payments" (low B2B search volume) but the wider space of **entrepreneurship** — digital literacy, financial literacy, compliance. And in fintech, where margins are in **basis points** and products are undifferentiated, relentless **cross-sell/upsell to preempt churn** (vs a Stripe poaching you) is survival.

## Key Terms to Remember

| Term | One-Line Definition |
|---|---|
| **SEO** | Search Engine Optimization — ranking organically on Google/search results |
| **GEO** | Generative Engine Optimization — getting your brand cited/recommended by AI LLMs |
| **Keyword Difficulty (KD)** | A 0–100 score of how hard it is to rank page one for a keyword |
| **Long-tail / flanking strategy** | Winning many low-volume, low-difficulty keywords instead of fighting for head terms |
| **Domain Authority (DA)** | A 0–100 score of how authoritative Google considers your whole domain |
| **Backlink** | A link from another site to yours; high-authority backlinks (e.g. Wikipedia) lift your DA |
| **Agentic automation** | Automation *with* memory + decision/discretionary power (vs plain rule-based automation) |
| **Earned media / brand mentions** | Third-party citations (PR, analysts, Reddit, YouTube) — the core driver of GEO |
| **Domain tools** | SEMrush, Moz, Ahrefs, Ubersuggest (keywords); SimilarWeb (traffic); App Annie (apps) |
| **AI content tools** | Rightsonic, Jasper, Leafpad (SEO content generation) |
| **Rain-maker / GTM advisor** | A senior industry figure who makes qualified intros for a variable cut of the deal |
| **Direct traffic** | Visitors typing your URL directly — a proxy for brand strength |

## The Real-World Takeaway

Riya ran marketing at a four-person analytics startup with a budget that could charitably be called "lunch money." Her investor kept forwarding her slick competitor campaigns and asking why she wasn't "doing something bold." So one week she tried: a burst of paid ads on the fattest keyword in her category. The clicks came. The money vanished. Conversions: almost none. She was standing in the same ring as billion-dollar incumbents, throwing punches at a wall.

Then she read about a travel company that had beaten giants with a budget of exactly zero. The lesson wasn't "work harder" — it was "fight somewhere else." She stopped chasing the one keyword everyone wanted and built fifty tiny pages for questions only her exact buyer would ever type. Low volume, low difficulty, quick page-one wins. Within four months, half her signups came from searches she'd never have thought worth owning.

But her real unlock came from a different direction. Her biggest prospect — a mid-market CFO — told her, almost offhand, "I asked ChatGPT about tools like yours, and you didn't come up." No ad budget would fix that. So Riya spent her evenings not writing copy but *earning mentions*: a founder's honest LinkedIn posts, a guest spot on a niche podcast, a real G2 review flow, one analyst who finally name-dropped her. She wasn't doing "GEO." She was doing what brand builders have always done — becoming the name people trust enough to repeat. The LLM just happened to be listening.

**In the digital world, the tool is never the strategy. SEO, GEO and AI content are just faster ways to earn the one thing that was always the point: being the brand worth mentioning when no one's being paid to mention you.**

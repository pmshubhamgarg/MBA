# EGS Exam Guide (Quick Revision)

Use this as your single high-signal revision sheet for the currently processed EGS sessions.

:::tip[How to use in 15 minutes]
1. Read Part 1 and Part 2 once.  
2. Practice Part 4 without looking.  
3. Before quiz/exam, revise Part 5 (answer structure).
:::

## 1) What to never forget (core logic)

1. Sustainability is a **strategy question**, not a side-function checklist.
2. Ownership sits with **board/CEO/business heads**; teams enable, leaders decide.
3. Risks and opportunities are linked; short-term fixes can create long-term fragility.
4. Double materiality improves quality, but without focus it creates overload.
5. If everything is priority, execution collapses and greenwashing risk rises.
6. Use four lenses to focus: purpose, stakeholders, business value, science/data.
7. Shared value means business value + societal value, together.
8. Sustainability survives only when institutionalized into normal operating rhythm.

## 2) Frameworks to write in answers (exam-ready)

### A) Materiality ladder

1. **Single materiality**: What affects company financial outcomes?
2. **Double materiality**: Plus, what impact does company action create externally?
3. **Focused materiality**: Which issues survive all four lenses and deserve current execution bandwidth?

### B) Four-lens prioritization model

For each candidate issue, test:

1. **Purpose fit** — aligned with what the company claims to stand for?
2. **Stakeholder criticality** — which stakeholder outcomes can block or accelerate execution?
3. **Business value** — clear linkage to risk reduction, resilience, growth, or trust?
4. **Science/data urgency** — evidence-backed urgency and actionability?

### C) Institutionalization loop

1. Convert priority issue into measurable operating objective.
2. Assign business ownership (not only specialist ownership).
3. Embed into planning, budgeting, sourcing, and governance reviews.
4. Track and adjust with stakeholder feedback.

## 4) Likely question angles

- Why sustainability must move from reporting to strategy
- Difference between single and double materiality
- How to prioritize when many material issues exist
- Role of board/CEO versus sustainability team
- How stakeholder engagement reduces reactive risk
- What “institutionalizing sustainability” means in practice

## 5) Fast self-check (answer in one line each)

1. Why is “everything is priority” dangerous?
2. What is one difference between single and double materiality?
3. Name the four lenses.
4. Why can’t sustainability remain only with one team?
5. What is shared value in business terms?
6. What changes when sustainability is institutionalized?

## 6) Answer structure (Never Freeze formula for EGS)

When stuck in a descriptive answer, write in this order:

1. **Define the concept in one plain line.**
2. **Apply one session framework** (materiality ladder or four lenses).
3. **Give one business scenario** (supplier, operations, capex, customer, compliance).
4. **State managerial action** (who owns it, where it gets embedded).
5. **Close with impact** (risk reduced, resilience improved, trust strengthened).

This keeps answers structured even if memory is partial.

# The Aarav Story (EGS Sessions 1-3)

*One connected story to lock the first three sessions into memory.*

---

Aarav had just become strategy lead at a fast-growing consumer company.  
On day one, he received a bright, polished ESG deck. Forty pages, twenty commitments, and five logos from external frameworks.

“Looks good,” he said.

His CFO smiled: “Looks good is easy. Can you tell me what we should do first?”

That question became Aarav’s turning point.

## Chapter 1: The illusion of activity (Session 1)

In the first leadership review, sustainability was discussed for six minutes and parked under “other updates.”  
Operations said cost pressure was high. Sales said customers were price-sensitive. Procurement said supplier shifts were risky.

Aarav noticed the pattern: everyone agreed sustainability mattered, but everyone treated it like someone else’s job.

He changed one slide in the next board meeting:

> “If this is strategy, ownership cannot sit only with one function.”

Silence. Then the CEO asked: “Fine. What exactly do we own?”

Aarav had his opening.

## Chapter 2: Twelve priorities, zero progress (Session 2)

The ESG team presented twelve “top priorities.”  
Each had logic. Each had urgency. Each had a passionate sponsor.

After one quarter, progress dashboards were mostly amber.  
Too many parallel starts. Too little depth.

Aarav proposed a filter workshop with four questions:

1. Does this align with our purpose?
2. Which stakeholders can block or accelerate it?
3. What business value does execution create?
4. What does evidence say about urgency and feasibility?

Twelve became four.

Some people felt disappointed.  
But for the first time, owners, timelines, and metrics were clear.

## Chapter 3: Campaign vs system (Session 3)

Momentum returned quickly.  
Teams celebrated. Internal mailers praised “ESG acceleration.”

Then quarter-end hit.  
Budget reviews got intense. Everyone moved to firefighting mode.

And sustainability work started slipping again.

Aarav realized the problem wasn’t motivation. It was design.  
The work still lived outside core operating loops.

So he embedded one sustainability checkpoint into each major rhythm:

- sourcing review
- capex approval
- risk dashboard
- monthly business review

No huge launch. No slogans. Just system placement.

By year-end, execution looked less glamorous and more real.

## What this story teaches

- Session 1: Don’t confuse visibility with ownership.
- Session 2: Don’t confuse issue volume with strategic focus.
- Session 3: Don’t confuse enthusiasm with institutionalization.

## Concept-to-story mapping

- **Strategy ownership** → CEO asking “What exactly do we own?”
- **Double materiality to focus** → Twelve priorities reduced to four through four lenses
- **Shared value + institutionalization** → ESG checkpoints embedded into business operating cadence

---

**Memory hook:** *If sustainability is outside business rhythm, it is a campaign. If it is inside rhythm, it becomes strategy.*

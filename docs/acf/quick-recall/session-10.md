# Session 10: Futures Pricing, Forwards and Introduction to Options

## The Story

You need office chairs in six months. Vendor A says, "Let's just agree on a price now, handshake deal, I'll deliver in six months" — informal, flexible, but if he backs out or goes out of business, you're stuck. Vendor B works through a big formal marketplace that *guarantees* the deal happens exactly as agreed — more rigid, but zero risk of someone just walking away. Then there's a third option: pay a small non-refundable fee now for the *right* to buy chairs at today's price in six months, but only use that right if prices actually go up.

## The Concept

That's forwards (private, flexible, but trust-dependent), futures (standardized, guaranteed, less flexible), and options (pay a small known cost for a right you only use if it benefits you).

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

Every negotiated future-dated deal you'll ever do — a vendor contract, a lease, a hiring pipeline — is really a choice between these three shapes. Knowing which one fits protects you from either being burned by an informal deal that falls apart, or overpaying for rigidity you didn't need.

## The One-Liner

*"A forward is a handshake, a future is a guarantee, and an option is a right you only use when it pays you to."*

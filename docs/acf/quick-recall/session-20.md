# Session 20: M&A Valuation Finale — Illiquidity Discounts and the Monmouth Corporation Case

## The Story

You're evaluating a small, family-run business — three generations old, no public shares, no easy way to just "sell out" if you change your mind. Even if it's identical in every way to a publicly listed competitor, it's worth *less*, purely because you can't easily cash out of it. You structure a fair deal anyway — part cash, part stock — closing the very last transaction of a long career built on one repeated question.

## The Concept

Illiquid, privately-held businesses deserve a discount simply for being hard to exit — and every single deal, all the way back to session one, comes back to the same first question: does this actually create value?

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

Anyone assessing a stake in a private company, a family business, or any asset that's hard to sell quickly needs to remember that "hard to exit" is itself a real cost — people who forget this consistently overpay for illiquid assets.

## The One-Liner

*"Every deal, no matter how complicated it gets, comes back to the same first question: does this actually create value?"*

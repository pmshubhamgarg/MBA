# Session 13: Interest Rate Swaps and Introduction to International Finance

## The Story

One freelancer friend has wildly unpredictable monthly income; a salaried friend has a stable but capped income. They jokingly agree: "Let's just average our incomes every month — you give me your extra good months, I'll cover your bad ones." Neither of them made more money overall — they just traded the *shape* of their risk for something each could live with better.

## The Concept

That's an interest rate swap. A company with a floating-rate loan doesn't swap to fixed to save money — it swaps because it wants **certainty** over unpredictability. And the moment any part of your business touches something international — an offshore vendor, a foreign subsidiary — you've inherited exposure whether you notice it or not.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

Any manager juggling variable costs (freelancers, offshore teams, floating-rate financing) is essentially living the swap problem — deciding whether certainty is worth paying for. And if you ever manage anything with an international thread, you need to know that invisible exposure exists even when nobody explicitly signed a currency deal.

## The One-Liner

*"A swap doesn't erase risk — it just finds someone willing to trade you the flavor of risk you can actually live with."*

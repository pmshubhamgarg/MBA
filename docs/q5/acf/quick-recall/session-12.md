# Session 12: Option Pricing and the Hidden Options in Every Balance Sheet

## The Story

A company is drowning in debt, barely surviving. Instead of playing it safe, management starts making wild, risky bets. Everyone calls it reckless. But look closer: if the bets fail, the company was going to zero anyway — the lenders eat that loss, not the owners. If the bets succeed, the owners keep every rupee of the upside. From where they're standing, the risky bet is actually the rational one.

## The Concept

Equity ownership behaves exactly like a call option on the whole company — unlimited upside, losses capped once you've got nothing left to lose. Debt is the mirror image — lenders absorb the downside beyond that point. This single insight explains a huge amount of "irrational-looking" behavior from struggling companies.

## Why This Matters as a Future Manager

This isn't just about finance — it explains behavior everywhere. A team with "nothing to lose" (a failing project close to being shut down) will often gamble hard, because the downside is already baked in. Recognizing this pattern helps you read incentives correctly instead of being surprised by seemingly reckless decisions.

## The One-Liner

*"Uncertainty doesn't just create risk — it creates the value of being able to change your mind, so always know what it costs you to keep that door open."*

# Managing in the Global Economy (MGE)

*Professor Partha Ray's "method course" — a way to think about money, policy, inflation and the exchange rate, rather than one fixed answer to memorise.*

---

## What this course is about

MGE is not a list of formulas to cram. Its spine is a single idea the professor repeats all term: in macroeconomics **the questions stay the same, but the answers keep changing** with ideology, context and the time period. So the first instinct to any "what is the right policy?" question is *it depends on the context* — then you argue where the dial should sit *this time*.

The sessions build in a deliberate order, each one adding a layer to the one before:

- **Session 1** draws the **map** — the whole global economy with no equations yet: comparative advantage, money as trust, why the dollar rules, and Asia's thousand-year rise.
- **Session 2** builds the **machine** — from demand and supply to the Keynesian cross, the multiplier, and the **IS–LM model**.
- **Session 3** **turns the knobs** — fiscal versus monetary policy, crowding out, the Budget and the fiscal deficit, and the RBI's actual toolkit.
- **Session 4** **bolts on prices** — derives aggregate demand from IS–LM, adds aggregate supply at two speeds, and meets the **inflation–output trade-off** ("no free lunch").
- **Session 8** **opens the economy** — the **Mundell–Fleming** model, where the same policy succeeds or fails depending on the exchange-rate regime, boxed in by the **Impossible Trinity**.

By the end the promise is modest but powerful: you will forget the exact equations in ten years, but you should keep the *method* — read the global board, ask "fixed or floating?", and make "a method of the madness."

## Sessions

| Session | Title | Key topics |
|---|---|---|
| [Session 1](session-01) | Where the Questions Stay the Same but the Answers Keep Changing | Macro as a **method**; two-way global–local linkage; comparative advantage; **money as trust** and central-bank independence; the **six channels** of globalization; **Nominal vs PPP GDP**; why the dollar rules (network externality); IMF vs World Bank |
| [Session 2](session-02) | In the Long Run We're All Dead — Rebuilding the Keynesian Machine | Three properties of equilibrium; the **Keynesian cross** (Y = C + I); **MPC less than 1**; the **multiplier** 1 / (1 − MPC); PDV; the **IS and LM curves**; Walras's Law; fiscal shifts IS, monetary shifts LM |
| [Session 3](session-03) | Money in Your Pocket, or a Word in Your Banker's Ear | Fiscal (**direct**) vs monetary (**indirect**); **crowding out** vs crowding in; the Budget and the **fiscal deficit**; FRBM 3% rule; the **repo corridor** (repo / SDF / MSF); CRR vs SLR; flexible inflation targeting; the six-member MPC |
| [Session 4](session-04) | The Short-Run Sugar Rush and the Long-Run Snap-Back | **Price vs price level**; inflation as a **rate of change**; deriving **AD from IS–LM**; **AS in two speeds** (vertical LRAS, upward SRAS); the short-run boost and long-run snap-back; demand management vs structural policy; the **Phillips curve** |
| [Session 8](session-08) | Same Policy, Opposite Endings — When the Exchange-Rate Regime Decides What Works | **Mundell–Fleming** (open-economy IS–LM); the small open economy (R = R-star); IS\* down, LM\* vertical; **policy effectiveness flips with the regime**; the exchange rate as a market price; the **Impossible Trinity** |

:::note[Sessions 5–7 are not yet available]

From the surrounding sessions we can see they sit in the gap where the course **opens the economy** — moving from Y = C + I + G to **Y = C + I + G + X − M**, bringing in exports, imports and the exchange rate, and *beginning* the Mundell–Fleming model that Session 8 completes. Those sessions are genuine gaps in these notes, not a summary — treat the jump from Session 4 to Session 8 accordingly.

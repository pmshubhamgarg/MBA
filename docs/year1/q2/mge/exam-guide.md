## Part 3 — Session-by-Session Rapid Recall

### Session 1 — The Map (description, no models)
- **Macro is "gray"** — the answer depends on **ideology, context and period**. Same questions, changing answers.
- **Two-way linkage** — shocks now flow both ways between advanced and emerging economies.
- **Comparative advantage** — do what you are *relatively* best at; trade deficits and outsourcing follow from it, not from charity or hostility.
- **Money as trust** — a note's value is trust in the state, **measured by inflation**; the central bank is kept **independent** so a re-election-seeking government cannot over-print.
- **Six channels of globalization** — goods, services, finance, technology, labour, regulation. (Finance is **scale-neutral** — a billion moves in one click.)
- **Nominal vs PPP GDP** — India ≈ **USD 3.9 trillion** nominal, ≈ **USD 16.2 trillion** PPP; only the **US** has the two equal.
- **Why the dollar rules** — an international currency does three jobs (**medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value**); the dollar holds its place by **network externality** ("A takes it because C will").
- **IMF vs World Bank** — IMF = short-term crisis, gives hard currency, charges **conditionality**; World Bank = long-term development projects.
- **Likely exam angles:** why a trade deficit is comparative advantage; why RBI, not the Finance Ministry; list the six channels; nominal vs PPP; the three functions of an international currency; IMF vs World Bank.

### Session 2 — The Machine (IS–LM from scratch)
- **Three properties of equilibrium** — **existence, stability, uniqueness** (examined mostly on the first two).
- **Keynesian cross** — a **one-commodity "day"** with **no price**; **quantity** clears and the shock-absorber is **inventory** (accumulate above E, draw down below E).
- **MPC less than 1** — you cannot consume more than you earn forever; this is a **stability condition**.
- **The multiplier** — dY / dG = **1 / (1 − MPC)**; with MPC = 0.5 it is **2** (₹1,000 crore of G becomes ₹2,000 crore of output). It converges *because* MPC is a fraction.
- **PDV** — future returns discounted by (1 + r); a **lower r lifts investment**, so **I depends negatively on r**.
- **IS curve** — commodity market, **I(r) + G = S(Y)**, **slopes down**, shifted by **G** (fiscal).
- **LM curve** — money market, **transaction (↑ with Y) + speculative (↓ with r) demand = M / P**, **slopes up**, shifted by **M** (monetary).
- **Walras's Law** — if n − 1 markets clear, the nth clears too (so we drop the bond market and keep IS and LM).
- **Likely exam angles:** state and explain the three properties; why no price in the cross and what adjusts; derive the multiplier; why MPC less than 1 does "double duty"; slope and shifter of IS and LM.

### Session 3 — Turning the Knobs
- **Fiscal is direct, monetary is indirect** — the government puts money in your **pocket** (G up or T down) and the multiplier does the rest; the RBI can only make your **banker's** life easy or hard, and the effect reaches you only if banks **transmit** it.
- **Two IS–LM shifts** — expansionary **fiscal: IS right → Y up, R up** (crowding out); expansionary **monetary: LM right → Y up, R down**.
- **Crowding out vs crowding in** — government borrowing lifts R and pushes private investment down; productive **infrastructure** can instead *pull in* private investment. "It depends."
- **The Budget constraint** — income = **taxes + PSU dividends + borrowing**; spending = **revenue** (no asset; biggest item is **salaries, wages, pensions**) vs **capital** (asset-creating).
- **Fiscal deficit = borrowing** — the single headline number; **FRBM target ≈ 3% of GDP** (4–5% tolerated, higher in a crisis). Keep **deficit (the year's flow) vs debt (the pile)** straight.
- **Repo corridor** — **MSF 5.75% / repo 5.50% / SDF 5.25%** (±25 bps); plus the blunt "sledgehammers" **CRR ≈ 3.75%** and **SLR ≈ 18%**.
- **Flexible inflation targeting** (since **2016**); rates set by a **six-member MPC**, not the Governor alone — the structural reason an election-year government cannot force a cut. Policy moves in **25-bps "baby steps"** because of the **transmission lag**.
- **Likely exam angles:** why monetary policy is slower/less reliable (**transmission**); explain crowding out and when it is acceptable; define the fiscal deficit and FRBM; label the repo corridor; CRR vs SLR; deficit vs debt.

### Session 4 — Bolting On Prices (AD–AS)
- **Price vs price level** — one good's price vs a **weighted index**; **WPI + indirect tax + trader margin = CPI**.
- **Inflation is a rate of change** — the price level can **rise while inflation falls** (100 → 110 → 120 is 10%, then ≈ 9.1%).
- **AD from IS–LM** — let **P** vary (only LM moves, via M / P); you get a **downward-sloping AD**, and **every point clears three markets** (commodity, money, bond — Walras's Law).
- **Demand management** — anything that shifts IS or LM shifts AD; that is all monetary and fiscal policy do.
- **AS in two speeds** — **LRAS is vertical at potential output Ȳ** (the production function); **SRAS slopes up**: **Y = Ȳ + α(P − Pᵉ)**, driven by price surprises; **Y − Ȳ is the output gap**.
- **Sugar rush → snap-back** — an expansionary push moves E → F (output up, price up), then expectations catch up and SRAS shifts, F → G (output **back to Ȳ**, price higher still).
- **No free lunch** — the **inflation–output trade-off**; overused it ends in hyperinflation (**Zimbabwe ≈ 1 million%/year**). **Inflation targeting** is the RBI's institutional "no."
- **Structural policy** raises Ȳ itself (education, infrastructure, institutions) — the only growth you can *keep*.
- **Phillips curve** — **π = πᵉ − β(u − uₙ)**; the **same relation as SRAS**, different axes; **uₙ** (natural rate of unemployment) ≈ **4–5%** (Friedman).
- **Likely exam angles:** price level vs inflation (the 100→110→120 trap); derive AD and name the three markets; draw LRAS and SRAS and write the SRAS equation; explain the snap-back and "no free lunch"; translate SRAS into the Phillips curve.

### Session 8 — Opening the Economy (Mundell–Fleming)
- **Small open economy** — a **price-taker** in world finance: **home rate = world rate (R = R-star)**; "small" is about financial power, **not size**. Prices are held fixed (nominal exchange rate E only).
- **New axes** — output **Y** against the exchange rate **E**: **IS\* slopes down**, **LM\* is vertical** (the rate is pinned to the world rate).
- **The exchange rate is a market price** — set where **supply of forex (S − I) = demand (NX)**; the RBI is a **big player, not the determiner**, smoothing within a **"Lakshman rekha."** Defending a **peg** means **shifting LM\*** by changing the money supply.
- **Policy effectiveness FLIPS with the regime** — under a **float**, monetary policy works and fiscal/trade policy don't; under a **peg**, fiscal and trade policy work and monetary policy doesn't.
- **Open-economy crowding out** — under a float, fiscal expansion crowds out **net exports** (the currency appreciates), not investment.
- **A tariff cannot shrink a deficit under a float** — imports fall, the currency appreciates, exports fall too → **less trade, same deficit**.
- **Capital chases the risk-adjusted real return** (nominal − inflation − country-risk θ); it is market-determined — India can suffer an outflow because a rating agency **re-scored the US**, through no fault of its own.
- **Impossible Trinity** — free capital flow + independent monetary policy + fixed exchange rate: **pick two**. (US drops the peg; Saudi drops its own money; China/old India drop free capital.)
- **Likely exam angles:** why "small open economy"; slopes of IS\* and LM\*; the policy-flip table; why a tariff fails under a float; draw and label the Impossible Trinity triangle with one country per edge.

## Part 4 — Frameworks You Can Draw From Memory

### The Keynesian Cross (one-commodity "day")
```
  Spending
    |                 45 deg line  (Y = Y, supply)
    |                /
    |               /
    |              /________  C + I  (aggregate demand)
    |             / E
    |            /:
    |           / :
    |__________/__:________________ Output (Y)
                 Y*
  Above E: supply beats demand -> inventory piles up -> cut output
  Below E: demand beats supply -> inventory drawn down -> raise output
  No price in the model; QUANTITY adjusts, inventory is the shock-absorber.
```

### The Multiplier
```
  Multiplier = 1 / (1 - MPC)
  MPC = 0.5  ->  1 / (1 - 0.5) = 2
  Extra G of Rs 1,000 crore  ->  Rs 2,000 crore of output
  Engine: each round spends a FRACTION (MPC) of the last, so echoes fade:
     1000 * (0.5 + 0.5^2 + 0.5^3 + ...) = 2000
  MPC less than 1 does double duty: stable equilibrium AND a converging sum.
```

### Consumption, PDV and why I falls with r
```
  Consumption function:  C = a + (MPC) Y ,  with 0 < MPC < 1
  PDV = R1/(1+r) + R2/(1+r)^2 + ... + Rn/(1+r)^n
  Lower r  ->  higher PDV  ->  more investment I
  So I depends NEGATIVELY on r  — the root of the downward-sloping IS curve.
```

### IS–LM (the spine)
```
  R |\ IS                 LM /
    | \                     /
    |  \                   /
    |   \                 /
    |    \       E       /
    |     \     o       /
    |      \   / \     /
    |       \ /   \   /
    |        X     \ /
    |       / \     X
    |______/___\___/_______________ Y
  IS (goods market): slopes DOWN, shifted by G   -> FISCAL policy
  LM (money market): slopes UP,   shifted by M   -> MONETARY policy
  Fiscal (G up):   IS right -> Y up, R up  (crowding out)
  Monetary (M up): LM right -> Y up, R down
```

### The Repo Corridor (RBI toolkit)
```
  MSF   5.75%   <- emergency borrowing, +25 bps, penal
  REPO  5.50%   <- the policy rate (the "magic number")
  SDF   5.25%   <- park surplus cash with RBI, -25 bps
  Corridor = repo +/- 25 bps   (1 basis point = 0.01%)
  Blunt "sledgehammers": CRR ~3.75% (cash with RBI), SLR ~18% (safe G-secs)
```

### AD–AS: Sugar Rush, then Snap-Back
```
  P |   LRAS (vertical at Y-bar)
    |    |              SRAS2
    |    |             /   SRAS1
    |    |            /   /
    |    |        G  o   /
    |    |          /. o F     (short-run boost: Y up, P up)
    |    |        /  /.
    |    |       /  / . 
    |    |      /  o E         (start: Y = Y-bar, P = P-expected)
    |    |     /  .  \
    |    |    /  .    \  AD2
    |    |   .        AD1
    |____|__________________________ Y
        Y-bar
  E -> F : expansionary push, AD shifts out  -> output up, price up
  F -> G : expectations catch up, SRAS shifts up -> output SNAPS BACK to Y-bar
  Long run: same output Y-bar, only a higher price.  No free lunch.
```

### The Phillips Curve (AS in disguise)
```
  inflation (pi)
     |  \
     |   \     pi = pi^e - beta (u - u_n)
     |    \
     |     \
     |      \
     |_______\________________ unemployment (u)
  Same relation as short-run AS, different axes:
    upward SRAS  <=>  downward Phillips curve
  u_n = natural rate of unemployment (~4-5%, Friedman); never zero.
```

### Mundell–Fleming (open economy) and the policy flip
```
  E |            LM* (vertical)
    |             |
    |          \  |
    |           \ |
    |            \| E-bar
    |             o
    |             |\
    |             | \  IS* (slopes down)
    |_____________|__\______________ Y
                 Y-bar
  Small open economy: home rate = world rate (R = R*)
  Defend a peg = shift LM* by changing the money supply

  POLICY EFFECTIVENESS FLIPS WITH THE REGIME
  --------------------------------------------------------
                     Floating (flexible)   Fixed (peg)
  Fiscal  (G up)     ineffective           effective
  Monetary (M up)    effective             no role
  Trade   (tariff)   ineffective           effective
  --------------------------------------------------------
  Memory: "Float loves Money; Peg loves G (and tariffs)."
```

### The Impossible Trinity (pick two)
```
                 Free capital flow
                       /  \
                      /    \
                     /      \
                    /        \
          Independent -------- Fixed exchange
          monetary policy       rate
  You can have at most TWO of the three:
    USA   : independent money + free capital   (gives up the peg; USD floats)
    Saudi : fixed rate + free capital          (gives up own money; mimics Fed)
    China : fixed-ish rate + some money control (gives up free capital)
    India (pre-1991): managed rate + own money  (blocked capital via FERA)
```

## Part 5 — Professor's Recurring Emphases

Highest-signal themes the professor returned to again and again:

1. **"The questions stay the same; the answers keep changing."** Macro is a *method*, so lead with *it depends on the context*.
2. **Question a model within the model** — judge internal consistency, not realism.
3. **Money is trust, measured by inflation** — which is why the central bank is kept **independent**.
4. **Fiscal is the pocket, monetary is the pipe** — and the **interest rate tells you which lever was pulled** (fiscal pushes R up, monetary pulls R down).
5. **There is no free lunch** — every short-run output gain is bought with higher prices.
6. **Demand moves AD; structure moves Ȳ** — only structural policy lifts potential output.
7. **Same policy, opposite endings** — in an open economy, *first ask "fixed or floating?"*
8. **You cannot have all three** — the Impossible Trinity constrains every central bank.

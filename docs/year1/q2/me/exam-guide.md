## Part 3 — Session-by-Session Rapid Recall

### Session 1 — One Fine Morning, You're Fired
- **Micro plans vs macro weather** — a firm has **total helplessness** over interest rates, taxes, inflation, exchange rates. Survival rule: **Understand → Anticipate → Adapt (be agile).**
- **Five macro assumptions** behind every plan: stable demand, interest, prices, taxes, exchange rates.
- **The cement story** — boom: demand 120 outruns capacity 100 → prices rise; bust: **sticky costs** won't fall, so firms shut rather than sell cheap.
- **Growth engine**: **Savings → Investment → Output**, filtered by the **Capital-Output Ratio**. Potential growth = savings ÷ COR = **36% ÷ 4 = 9%**.
- **AD vs AS**: inflation turns dangerous only when **price rise is greater than output growth**.
- **Toolkit**: fiscal (FM — government spending, taxes) + monetary (RBI — repo, CRR, SLR); **Keynes's pump priming** in a depression.
- **Business cycle** — Peak → Recession → Depression → Recovery; intensity/length unpredictable; **shipping is a leading indicator**; postponable purchases = high **beta** (cyclical).
- **Likely angles:** why a firm can't control macro variables; compute potential growth from savings and COR; when is inflation dangerous; name the four phases.

### Session 2 — Follow the Rupee
- **GDP definition** (every word loaded): total **market value** of all **final** goods and services **produced within a country** in a **period**.
- **Circular flow** — households and firms linked by the **factor market** and **product market**; stand anywhere and measure the same loop.
- **Three methods**: Product = Income = Expenditure; **Expenditure = C + I + G + (X − M)**.
- **Final vs intermediate** goods; counting both = **double counting**. **Market value = price × quantity.**
- **Leakages** (savings, taxes, imports) must be matched by **injections** (investment, government spending, exports).
- **Per-capita GDP** — India about **USD 2,697**, ranked **137th**, even though total GDP is 4th–5th (**USD 3–3.5 trillion**).
- **Likely angles:** state the definition; draw the circular flow; why the three methods are equal; final vs intermediate.

### Session 3 — One Loaf of Bread (Agraria)
- **Value added = output − purchased inputs**, summed across firms = GDP; it avoids double counting when you can't tell final from intermediate. **Agraria = ₹290.**
- The **grinder** is a **final (capital) good** — added, not netted out; **imports are subtracted**; **exports and government are just more buyers.**
- **Expenditure identity Y = C + G + I + (X − M)** lands on the same ₹290.
- **Transfer payments** (pensions, no service this year) are **excluded** from the product method.
- **Nominal vs real** — the **coconut** test: 20% nominal, **10% real**; real GDP holds prices at a **base year** ("a normal year").
- **Limits of GDP** — informal + black economy (underestimate), ignores well-being/environment/inequality, the innovation paradox; **Happiness Index** (Finland, Bhutan).
- **Likely angles:** compute GDP by value added; why value added beats "spot the final good"; back out real growth from nominal + price; five limitations of GDP.

### Session 4 — Why Falling Inflation Still Raises Your Bill
- **Three rulers**: **CPI** (retail, consumer, includes services), **WPI** (wholesale, goods only, no services), **GDP deflator** (whole economy, current-year weights).
- **Build a CPI**: basket → base-year **weights** (held fixed) → **price relatives** → weighted sum. **CPI = Σ (weight × price relative)**; the worked basket lands near **121** (about 21% above base).
- **GDP deflator = Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP × 100** — comprehensive, but arrives with a **one-year lag**.
- **Inflation = rate of change** of the price level. **Falling inflation ≠ falling prices**; only negative inflation (**deflation**) means prices fall. Low inflation can hide **weak demand**.
- **Demand-pull** (AD up → more output, mild price rise — manageable) vs **cost-push** (AS inward via wages/oil → output falls, prices jump — hard).
- **Stagflation** = stagnation + inflation; the wage–price spiral.
- **Likely angles:** build a CPI from a basket; deflator formula; "inflation down but prices up — explain"; demand-pull vs cost-push.

### Session 5 — The Economy's Vital Signs
- **Don't trust one number** — read inflation alongside investment, growth, exports, even shipping.
- **Unemployment**: labour force = employed + actively seeking; **unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ labour force × 100**; **LFPR = labour force ÷ adult population × 100**.
- **Four types**: frictional, seasonal, structural (all inside the **natural rate**) and **cyclical** — the one policymakers chase.
- **Okun's Law** — 1 point of extra unemployment → about **2% GDP** below potential; the exception is **jobless growth** (automation/AI).
- **GDP family**: **GDP = geography, GNP = people**, bridged by **NFIA** (factor income in − out: wages, rent, interest, profit). India's GNP is slightly **below** GDP → **NFIA negative**.
- **Three accounting axes**: Domestic↔National (**+ NFIA**), Gross↔Net (**− Depreciation**), Market price↔Factor cost (**− indirect taxes + subsidies**). **National Income = NNP at factor cost.**
- **Likely angles:** classify an unemployment type; apply Okun; GDP vs GNP and the sign of NFIA; convert across the three axes.

### Session 6 — One Identity, Two Deficits
- **Y = C + I + G + X − M**, and in equilibrium **S = I** ("say it in your sleep").
- **Blow up savings**: **S private = Y − T − C**, **S government = T − G**, **S row = M − X**; together **= I**.
- **Sign rules** (the trap): **S government (T − G)** → plus good, minus dangerous; **S row (M − X)** → **plus = current account deficit = DANGER**, minus = safe surplus.
- **Twin deficit** — a fiscal deficit forces a current account deficit if investment (hence growth) is to hold; only safe while **foreign savings keep flowing in** (fine for the US, risky for India).
- **A deficit is not automatically bad** — it is fine if **sustainable**. **FRBM** caps India's fiscal deficit (about 3–4%); no fiscal surplus since roughly 1970.
- **Likely angles:** derive S = I; the three savers and their formulas; the sign rules; define the twin deficit with the US-vs-India contrast.

### Session 7 — When India Mortgaged Its Gold
- **1991** = the twin deficit at its most brutal: current account deficit, foreign inflows dried up, **mortgaged gold**, IMF loan, **forced devaluation**, then reform (flexible rate + **convertibility** + open economy).
- **Fixed vs flexible** rate — fixed gives certainty but must be defended with reserves; India moved to a **managed float**. **Pegging**: Nepal about 1.6, Bhutan 1:1.
- **Aggregate demand Y = C + I + G + (X − M)**; its swings drive the business cycle.
- **Micawber principle** — live within your means; **S = I** is the engine of growth.
- **Consumption function C = A + cY**: A = autonomous consumption; **c = MPC = ΔC/ΔY**, always between 0 and 1, higher for the poor. **S = −A + (1 − c)Y**; **MPC + MPS = 1**.
- **Investment** depends on **interest rates + business expectations** (volatile); **government spending** is the autonomous rescue lever.
- **Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)** (MPC 0.5 → 2, MPC 0.8 → 5).
- **Likely angles:** narrate the 1991 chain; fixed vs flexible; write C = A + cY and define MPC; state the multiplier.

### Session 8 — One Rupee In, Three Rupees Out
- **AD = C + I + G** (closed economy); derive **Y₀ = (a − cT + I + G) × 1 ÷ (1 − c) = 1 ÷ (1 − c) × A**.
- **Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − c) = 1 ÷ MPS**; the **ripple** is a geometric series: ₹1,000 at MPC 0.667 → 1,000 + 666.7 + 444.4 + … = **₹3,000**.
- **Government-spending multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − c)**; **tax multiplier = −c ÷ (1 − c)** (negative, one step weaker — at c = 0.8 it is **−4**); **balanced-budget multiplier = 1** (always, whatever c).
- **Higher MPC → bigger multiplier**, so developing economies (India's MPC about 0.7–0.8) grow faster from each injection; in a slump you **pump prime**.
- Wars = big autonomous **G** → big GDP jumps (the defence-spending table), except when fear-driven exogenous factors offset it (Persian Gulf went negative).
- **Three limitations**: no monetary policy, no rest of the world, no supply side (justified only by the depression's horizontal supply).
- **Likely angles:** derive Y₀ and the multiplier; explain the ripple; compute spending/tax/balanced-budget multipliers; why the balanced budget equals 1.

### Session 9 — How ₹100 Becomes ₹1,000
- **Money vs wealth = liquidity**; money's three jobs (medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value). Ladder: **barter → commodity → fiat** (legal tender by government decree).
- **Velocity** moves transactions without creating money; **fractional reserve** lending **does** create money.
- **₹100 → ₹90 → ₹81 …** with R = 10% sums to **₹1,000**; **money multiplier = 1 ÷ R**.
- **M0 = high-powered money** (currency + reserves at the RBI) — the base the RBI controls; bankers care about **M1 and M3**.
- **Modified multiplier = (CU/D + 1) ÷ (CU/D + R)** — with CU/D = 0.2 and R = 0.1 it is **4**, so ₹100 supports only **₹400**. The RBI **cannot control CU/D** (public trust).
- **Toolkit**: **CRR** (cash), **SLR** (government securities, "gilt"), **OMO** (buy = inject, sell = suck out), **repo/reverse repo** (short-term), **bank rate** (signal).
- **Likely angles:** draw the ₹100 chain and state 1 ÷ R; why fractional reserve creates money; the modified multiplier; CRR vs SLR; OMO vs repo.

### Session 10 — How ₹100 Becomes ₹400
- Recap the **money multiplier** (simple 1 ÷ r and modified) and **M = multiplier × H**; **M0 = monetary base = reserve money**; the **M1–M4** liquidity ladder.
- **Three money-demand motives**: transactions and precautionary (rise with **income**); **speculative** (inverse to the **interest rate**). **Bond prices and interest rates are inversely related.**
- **Liquidity trap** — at rock-bottom rates money demand is huge/infinite; pump in money and people sit on it, so policy loses grip.
- **Value of money = 1 ÷ P**; money supply is a vertical line; shifting it right lowers the value of money → **prices rise**.
- **Quantity theory MV = PY** → back out **V = PY ÷ M**; with V and Y roughly constant in the short run, **inflation ≈ growth rate of money supply** — "inflation is a monetary phenomenon" (Friedman). Keynes/fiscal rules a recession; monetarists/money rule normal times.
- **Expansionary vs restrictive** policy; India today — inflation in band, weak investment → expansionary justified.
- **Likely angles:** write MV = PY and derive inflation ≈ money growth; explain the liquidity trap; value-of-money equilibrium; Keynes vs Friedman.

## Part 4 — Frameworks You Can Draw From Memory

### Circular flow and the three methods
```text
        FACTOR MARKET  (land, labour, capital, skills)
        firms pay rent / wages / interest  ->  households
   HOUSEHOLDS  <==============================>  FIRMS
        households spend  <-  PRODUCT MARKET  ->  firms sell
                  final goods and services

  Stand anywhere on the loop -> the SAME economy:
    PRODUCT      = value of all final goods produced
    INCOME       = wages + rent + interest + profit   (= national income)
    EXPENDITURE  = C + I + G + (X - M)
  Three roads, one number:  PRODUCT = INCOME = EXPENDITURE

  Leak OUT : Savings , Taxes , Imports
  Plug BACK: Investment , Govt spending , Exports
```

### Value added (Agraria = ₹290)
```text
  VALUE ADDED = output - purchased inputs   (summed = GDP, no double counting)

  Radha  (wheat)    100 - 0                        = 100
  Ravi   (flour)    200 - 140 (wheat 100 + import 40) =  60
  Gopal  (grinder)   30 - 0                         =  30  <- capital good = FINAL
  Reika  (bread)    300 - 200 (flour)              = 100
                                              GDP  = 290

  Expenditure check:  C 250 + G 30 + I 30 + X 20 - M 40 = 290
```

### Real vs nominal, and the deflator
```text
  Nominal GDP  = current quantities x CURRENT prices
  Real GDP     = current quantities x BASE-year prices
  GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) x 100

  Coconut economy:
    2020:  GDP 2,000 @ price 10.00  -> 200 coconuts
    2021:  GDP 2,400 @ price 10.91  -> ~220 coconuts
    Nominal growth 20%  BUT real (quantity) growth only 10%
```

### CPI and inflation
```text
  CPI = SUM ( weight x price relative )
        weight         = item base-year spend / total base-year spend  (FIXED)
        price relative = (current price / base price) x 100
  Shortcut: SUM(current price x base qty) / SUM(base price x base qty) x 100

  INFLATION = rate of CHANGE of the price level (NOT the level):
        inflation(yr2) = (index2 - index1) / index1 x 100
  6% -> 5% : prices STILL rise, just slower.  Falling prices = deflation.
```

### AD vs AS: demand-pull, cost-push, stagflation
```text
  DEMAND-PULL : aggregate demand (C + G + I + X - M) rises
                -> output UP, prices up a little     (manageable: cool demand)
  COST-PUSH   : costs rise (wages / oil), supply shifts INWARD
                -> output DOWN, prices up sharply    (hard: mostly external)
  STAGFLATION : cost-push pushed hard -> output falls WHILE prices climb
                wage -> price -> wage spiral ; prevent, don't cure
```

### The business cycle
```text
  output
    |     peak                         peak
    |    /    \        recovery        /
    |---/------\----------/-----------/----- potential (~9%)
    |  /        \        /
    | /          \      /  <- depression / trough
    |/____________\____/____________________ time
  Peak -> Recession -> Depression (trough) -> Recovery
  Draw the wave, but you CANNOT predict its intensity or length.
  Postponable purchase -> cyclical (beta greater than 1)
  Can't postpone (food) -> smooth (beta less than 1)
```

### The GDP family (three accounting axes)
```text
  DOMESTIC   --(+ NFIA)-------------------->  NATIONAL     (GDP -> GNP)
  GROSS      --(- Depreciation)------------>  NET
  MARKET price --(- indirect taxes + subsidies)-> FACTOR cost

  NFIA = factor income (wages, rent, interest, profit) IN minus OUT
  National Income = NNP at factor cost
  Assume all three away (closed economy) -> GDP = GNP = NNP = NI
```

### Consumption function and the 45-degree line
```text
  spend |              45-degree line (C = Y, MPC = 1)
        |             /
        |            /          C = A + cY
        |           /        __/
        |          /      __/    slope c = MPC = dC/dY , 0 < c < 1
        |         /    __/
        |        / __/   A  <- autonomous consumption (intercept)
        |______/_______________________ income (Y)
   Above the 45 line = dissaving ; below = saving.
   Saving: S = -A + (1 - c)Y ;  MPC + MPS = 1
```

### The multiplier and its fiscal cousins
```text
  MULTIPLIER = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS
     MPC 0.5 -> 2 ,  MPC 0.667 -> 3 ,  MPC 0.8 -> 5 ,  MPC 0.9 -> 10
  Ripple:  dI + c.dI + c^2.dI + ... = dI x 1/(1 - c)
     Rs 1,000 @ MPC 0.667 -> 1,000 + 666.7 + 444.4 + ... = Rs 3,000

  Government-spending multiplier = 1 / (1 - c)
  Tax multiplier                = -c / (1 - c)   (negative, one step weaker)
  Balanced budget (dG = dT)     = 1   (always 1, whatever c)
```

### S = I and the twin deficit
```text
  Equilibrium:  Y = C + I + G + X - M   <=>   S = I
  Blow up savings:
     S private    = Y - T - C
     S government = T - G     (+ surplus GOOD , - deficit BAD)
     S row        = M - X     (+ current account deficit = DANGER , - safe)
  S private + S government + S row = I

  TWIN DEFICIT: a fiscal deficit (S gov minus) forces a current account
  deficit (S row plus) if investment I is to hold -> only safe while
  foreign savings keep flowing in.
```

### The money multiplier
```text
  Fractional reserve: keep fraction R, lend the rest -> money is CREATED
     Rs 100 -> keep 10, lend 90 -> deposit 90 -> keep 9, lend 81 -> ...
  Simple multiplier    = 1 / R        (R = 0.10 -> x10 -> Rs 100 = Rs 1,000)
  Modified (cash leak) = (CU/D + 1) / (CU/D + R)
     CU/D = 0.2 , R = 0.1 -> 1.2 / 0.3 = 4 -> Rs 100 = only Rs 400
  Money supply  M = multiplier x H  (H = high-powered money = M0 = CU + R)
  RBI controls R and H ; it CANNOT control CU/D (public trust).
```

### The quantity theory (MV = PY)
```text
  M V = P Y      M money , V velocity , P price level , Y real output
  P Y = nominal GDP .   Back out velocity:  V = P Y / M
  Short run: V and Y roughly CONSTANT  =>  a change in M flows into P
     inflation ~= growth rate of money supply   ("inflation is monetary")
  Value of money = 1 / P.  More money -> value of money falls -> prices rise.
  Keynes (recession: fiscal + multiplier)  vs  Friedman (normal times: money)
```

## Part 6 — The Professor's Recurring Emphases

Highest-signal refrains across the ten sessions:

1. **You can't control the macro weather** — only **Understand → Anticipate → Be agile.**
2. **Memorise the GDP definition word for word** — "every single word has a meaning."
3. **Product = Income = Expenditure** — make it, spend it, earn it; the same number three ways.
4. **Take GDP with a pinch of salt** — it misses the informal + black economy, well-being, the environment and inequality.
5. **Inflation is a rate, not a level** — and **never read one macro number in isolation.**
6. **Y = C + I + G + X − M and S = I** — "you should be able to say that in your sleep."
7. **Live within your means** (Micawber) — and savings, routed through markets, become investment.
8. **A small injection, a magnified result** — in a slump, **G is the lever** and a high MPC makes it work harder.
9. **When trust breaks, the multiplier breaks** — the RBI can set the stage, not force the play.
10. **Inflation is a monetary phenomenon** (Friedman) — but Keynes and fiscal policy rule a recession.

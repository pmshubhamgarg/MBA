# Managerial Economics (ME)

*Despite the name, this is a **macroeconomics** course — GDP, inflation, jobs, deficits, the multiplier and money creation — taught as the "weather" no single business can control, but every manager has to learn to read.*

---

## What this course is about

ME answers one question a manager can never escape: **what happens to my plans when the whole economy moves, and I can do nothing to stop it?** You decide price, demand and output on your own drawing table — then a tariff, a rate hike or a Budget blows the plan apart. The firm optimises *within* the macro environment; it cannot *set* it.

Across ten sessions the professor builds the macro toolkit from the ground up:

- **why** macro shocks are real, uncontrollable and financial — and the only survival rule, **understand → anticipate → be agile** (Session 1)
- **what** GDP is and the **three equal ways** to measure it — product, income, expenditure (Sessions 2–3)
- how to measure the **price level** (CPI, WPI, GDP deflator) and turn it into **inflation** — a rate, not a level — alongside unemployment and the GDP family (Sessions 4–5)
- the **savings = investment** identity and the **twin deficit** that ties a budget hole to a trade hole, with 1991 as the warning (Sessions 6–7)
- Keynes's **multiplier** — how one rupee of spending becomes many (Sessions 7–8)
- how banks **create money** and the RBI steers it, closing on "**inflation is a monetary phenomenon**" (Sessions 9–10)

It is, in the professor's own words, "a rushed course" of ten sessions — so these notes keep the vocabulary tight, drill the handful of formulas that matter, and lean on the stories she told in class.

## Sessions

| Session | Title | Key topics |
|---|---|---|
| [Session 1](session-01) | One Fine Morning, You're Fired | Micro vs macro; "total helplessness"; **understand → anticipate → be agile**; five macro assumptions; **Savings = Investment**, capital-output ratio, **36% ÷ 4 = 9%**; AD vs AS; fiscal (FM) + monetary (RBI); the business cycle; Keynes & pump priming |
| [Session 2](session-02) | Follow the Rupee — How an Economy Counts Itself | The GDP definition; the **circular flow**; factor vs product market; the **three methods** (product, income, expenditure); **C + I + G + (X − M)**; final vs intermediate goods; double counting; leakages & injections; nominal vs real; per-capita GDP |
| [Session 3](session-03) | One Loaf of Bread, Three Ways to Count a Country | **Agraria = ₹290**; the **value-added method**; **Y = C + G + I + (X − M)**; transfer payments excluded; **nominal vs real** (the coconut test); the base year; the limits of GDP (Happiness Index, informal + black economy) |
| [Session 4](session-04) | Why Falling Inflation Still Raises Your Bill | **CPI, WPI, GDP deflator**; building a CPI by hand; **GDP deflator = Nominal ÷ Real × 100**; **inflation = the rate of change** of the price level; demand-pull vs cost-push; stagflation; the repo rate |
| [Session 5](session-05) | The Economy's Vital Signs — Why One Number Always Lies | The three price gauges; four types of **unemployment** and the natural rate; **unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ labour force**; **Okun's Law** (1 point up → about 2% GDP down); **GDP vs GNP** and **NFIA**; the three accounting axes; National Income = NNP at factor cost; jobless growth |
| [Session 6](session-06) | One Identity, Two Deficits | **Y = C + I + G + X − M**; the **S = I** identity; **S private = Y − T − C**, **S government = T − G**, **S row = M − X**; the **twin deficit**; the sign rules; the FRBM cap |
| [Session 7](session-07) | When India Mortgaged Its Gold — and Keynes Lit the Multiplier | The **1991 crisis** (IMF, gold, devaluation, reforms); fixed vs flexible exchange rate; aggregate demand; the **consumption function C = A + cY**; **MPC**, with MPC + MPS = 1; the **multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)**; the Micawber principle |
| [Session 8](session-08) | One Rupee In, Three Rupees Out — The Keynesian Multiplier | **AD = C + I + G**; equilibrium **Y₀ = 1 ÷ (1 − c) × A**; the **ripple** (geometric series); the government-spending multiplier; the **tax multiplier = −c ÷ (1 − c)**; the **balanced-budget multiplier = 1**; wars and pump priming |
| [Session 9](session-09) | How ₹100 Becomes ₹1,000 — Inside the Money Machine | Barter → commodity → fiat money; the **fractional reserve system**; the **money multiplier = 1 ÷ R**; **high-powered money (M0)**; M1 and M3; the **modified multiplier** (the cash leak); CRR, SLR, OMO, repo |
| [Session 10](session-10) | How ₹100 Becomes ₹400 — and Why Inflation Is Always Monetary | Simple vs modified money multiplier; **M = multiplier × H**; the money-demand motives and the **liquidity trap**; **value of money = 1 ÷ P**; the quantity theory **MV = PY**; **inflation ≈ money-supply growth**; expansionary vs restrictive policy |

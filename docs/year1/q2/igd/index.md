# Individual and Group Dynamics (IGD)

*An Organizational Behaviour course that answers one deceptively simple question — **why do two people in the same office, under the same boss, behave so differently?** — and then teaches you to read those differences in yourself, your colleagues and your organization.*

---

## What this course is about

IGD starts from a single claim the professor repeats all term: **no two people are the same, so one-size-fits-all never works with human beings.** The difference lives in your **P-factors** — personality, values, attitudes, biases, motivation and emotions — and learning to read them is the real job of a manager. The whole course is a three-step zoom-out: **Self → Others → Organization.**

Across twenty sessions (taught over Zoom to a batch of working professionals, with no midterm) the professor builds the toolkit from the inside out:

- **why people differ** — the P-factors, and **Behaviour = f(Person, Environment)** (Kurt Lewin); nature + nurture + memes; VABs, attitudes and the **EVLN** responses to dissatisfaction (Sessions 1–3)
- **the case method and influence without authority** — Michael Peterson and Amelia Rogers, two technically brilliant people undone by weak people skills (Sessions 2, 4)
- **personality** — the **MBTI** four-letter code, the **Big Five / OCEAN**, and the shadow-side **Dark Triad** (Sessions 5–7)
- **perception and bias** — "if you have a brain, you have biases," from attribution errors to the halo effect (Session 8)
- **motivation** — the **content** theories (Maslow, Herzberg, McClelland, Self-Determination) and the **process** theories (goal-setting, equity, expectancy, self-efficacy, reinforcement) (Sessions 9–10)
- **emotions and EQ** — six universal emotions and why **EQ outranks IQ** for how far you rise (Session 11)
- **teams** — the **Four C's** and the three instruments that diagnose them: Belbin, FIRO and Lencioni (Sessions 12–13)
- **conflict, negotiation, power and politics** — Thomas–Kilmann styles, French and Raven's power bases, and **political skill** (Sessions 14–15)
- **the manager-to-leader transition** — executive coaching and the full leadership playbook (Sessions 16–17)
- **ethics** — three ways to judge right from wrong (Session 18), before the Big Five project presentations close the course (Sessions 19–20)

It is, as the professor keeps saying, a "soft" subject full of theories for a reason: a theory is just a set of ideas that explains human behaviour, and it applies to everyone.

## Sessions

| Session | Title | Key topics |
|---|---|---|
| [Session 1](session-01) | Same Office, Two Very Different People | **No two people are the same**; **P-factors**; **Self → Others → Organization**; intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation; **IQ vs EQ (20/80)**; perception is not reality; Herzberg & goal-setting as theories; the case-cracking steps |
| [Session 2](session-02) | Nature, Nurture, and the Equation Behind How You Behave | Nature vs nurture (**interactionist view**); **Behaviour = f(Person, Environment)**; the OB vocabulary (OCB, voice, CWB, turnover); attitude vs behaviour; the **Michael Peterson** case |
| [Session 3](session-03) | Ten Percent Visible, Ninety Percent Hidden | The **behaviour iceberg** (10% / 90%); **Nature + Nurture + Memes**; **VABs** (core vs peripheral); the **ABC model** of attitudes; **EVLN** (Exit · Voice · Loyalty · Neglect) |
| [Session 4](session-04) | The Phone Call That Exposed a People Problem | The full case method; **influence without authority** (consultation, inspirational appeal); micromanagement; **technical skill + people skill**; the **Amelia Rogers / Tassani** case |
| [Session 5](session-05) | Four Letters That Explain Why You Are You | Personality defined (**enduring**); **MBTI** — four pairs (E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P) → 16 types; self-assessment biases (SOAP); blind spots; MBTI vs Big Five |
| [Session 6](session-06) | Five Castaways, One OCEAN | **Big Five / OCEAN** (5 traits × 6 sub-traits); the **defining trait**; the levels organizations want; **maturity principle**; trait activation vs situation strength |
| [Session 7](session-07) | The Villain in the Mirror — Your Dark Triad | The **Dark Triad** (Machiavellianism, Narcissism, Psychopathy); **emergence vs effectiveness**; workplace traits; personality disorders; the **Jensen Shoes** bridge |
| [Session 8](session-08) | If You Have a Brain, You Have Biases | Memes → VABs → biases; **attribution theory**; **FAE vs self-serving bias**; overconfidence; the **halo/horn, contrast, anchoring** catalogue; the **Jensen Shoe Company** case |
| [Session 9](session-09) | The Man Who Said No to a Raise | Intrinsic vs extrinsic vs amotivation; why carrot-and-stick fails; **content theories** — Herzberg, Maslow, McClelland, **Self-Determination (ACR)**; crowding-out effect |
| [Session 10](session-10) | Two Teams, One Roof | **Process theories** — Goal-Setting (SMART), Equity, **Expectancy** (E→P→R), Self-Efficacy, Reinforcement; the Motivation Toolkit; the **Crutchfield** case; **B = f(P, E)** |
| [Session 11](session-11) | The Snake Under the Table — Why EQ Outranks IQ | **Discrete Emotions Theory** (six universal emotions); **EQ vs IQ**; **Goleman's five components**; empathy vs sympathy; emotional labor; emotional contagion; Affective Events Theory |
| [Session 12](session-12) | Everyone's Competent — So Why Does the Team Fail? | The **Four C's** (Competence, Capabilities, Compatibility, Cohesion); **Belbin**; **FIRO-B**; **Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions**; team synergy; the Desert Survival exercise |
| [Session 13](session-13) | Smart People, Failing Teams — the Three Tests | Three psychometric instruments: **Belbin** (9 roles / 3 families), **FIRO** (Inclusion-Control-Affection, Expressed vs Wanted), **Five Dysfunctions** (trust first); collective intelligence |
| [Session 14](session-14) | My Way, Your Way, or a Better Way | Conflict (functional vs dysfunctional; task/relationship/process); the **Thomas–Kilmann** five styles; **distributive vs integrative** negotiation; third-party roles; the **TKC Consulting** case |
| [Session 15](session-15) | Politics Is the Water, Political Skill Is Your Swimming | Power = capacity to influence; **French and Raven's five bases**; organizational politics; **political skill** (four dimensions); the **Caroline Reges / Excel** case; LMX |
| [Session 16](session-16) | The Star Who Was Told She Couldn't Lead | **Executive coaching** ("unblock potential, don't fix problems"); the Birkman Method; three practices (**Relational, Independent, Self-generation**); the **Coaching McKenna Lane** case |
| [Session 17](session-17) | Born or Made? The Leader Who Changes Gears | Leadership as a **trainable skill**; three theories (**trait / behavioural / contingency**); the Managerial Grid; the **Full Range Model** and **Four I's**; Lewin's styles; authentic/ethical/servant; abusive supervision |
| [Session 18](session-18) | The Trolley, the Fat Man, and Three Ways to Judge Right | Ethics vs morality; ethical dilemmas; **Utilitarianism** (consequences), **Deontology** (action/duty), **Virtue Ethics** (character); plus the **end-term format** shared in class |
| [Session 19](session-19) | The Mirror That Spoke Back | Big Five project presentations (night 1); the two-stage method; **perception is not reality**; loud vs quiet traits; complementary opposites; the **contrast effect** *(lighter — project night)* |
| [Session 20](session-20) | The Mirror Test — Knowing Yourself Needs a Second Opinion | Big Five presentations (night 2) and close; **triangulation**; blind spots; traits enduring but behaviour workable; the professor's **exam and grading guidance** *(lighter — project night)* |

> **Quick revision:** the [**Exam Guide**](exam-guide) is the single page to open before the end-term — the whole course compressed, every framework as a simple diagram, all the cases in one place, and a last-night checklist.

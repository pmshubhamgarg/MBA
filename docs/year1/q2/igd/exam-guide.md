## Part 1 — The Exam at a Glance

### What we actually know about assessment

IGD is graded on coursework plus an end-term; **there is no midterm** (confirmed several times in class). The components the professor named:

| Component | Weightage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Class Participation (CP)** | 20% | **Normalised to a 6-to-9 band** — minimum 6, maximum 9 |
| **Group personality project (Big Five)** | 40% | Runs in **three stages**; it is the course's biggest single piece |
| **End-term examination** | 20% | Closed book, descriptive, short case scenarios |
| **Group presentations** | *(weightage not stated numerically)* | One presentation per study group, starting Session 3; scored on content, clarity, structure, creativity, delivery |

:::note[Good to Know — on the numbers]
The professor stated CP at **20%**, the personality project at **40%**, and the end-term at **20%** — that is 80% explicitly named. The **exact weightage of the group presentations was not given numerically** in the sessions, so do not assume it; just know presentations are graded and matter. Also: **you essentially cannot score a perfect 10** in IGD because it is a "soft," subjective subject, which is exactly why CP is compressed to the **6–9** band.
:::

### The end-term (format shared in Session 18)

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
The IGD end-term is **closed-book**, about **2 hours**, and likely **pen-and-paper**. It has **4 questions** — **2 drawn from pre-midterm sessions and 2 from post-midterm sessions.** The questions are **short case scenarios** you analyse using the relevant IGD concept; allow about **two pages / half an hour per answer.** There may also be a few **MCQs or one-word answers** plus a choice such as **two of three case studies.** The professor's own tip: *"Everything is in the question itself — if you know what a concept means, you identify the instances in the case and write your answer. If you don't know the concept, you can't answer."*
:::

The **sample question** the professor shared asked students to **evaluate a protagonist's political skill across its four sub-dimensions** (social astuteness, interpersonal influence, networking ability, apparent sincerity) using instances from the case. That is the exact shape to prepare for: **know each key term in one crisp line, then mine the case for a matching instance.**

### The personality project (40%)

The capstone runs in three stages and uses **triangulation** — never one data point:

| Stage | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — Peer interview & observation** | Interview/observe teammates; tag which OCEAN trait each most embodies (the **qualitative** view) |
| **Stage 2 — Self-test vs peer perception** | Take the Big Five test, compare your self-score to how peers rate you (the **quantitative** view, and the **gap** = blind spots) |
| **Stage 3 — Consolidated report** | Write up the combined, triangulated portrait |

### What to revise from

> **The professor (Session 20):** revise from **her PPT slide decks** — that is the material the exam is built on, not outside reading.

- **EQ outranks IQ**; Goleman's five components; empathy vs sympathy (S11)
- **Four C's** of teams + Belbin / FIRO / Lencioni (S12–13)
- **Thomas–Kilmann** five conflict styles + distributive vs integrative negotiation (S14)

### The individual (Sessions 1–11)

**Session 1 — Same Office, Two Different People.** No two people are the same, so **one-size-fits-all fails**; the **P-factors** (personality, VABs, attitudes, biases, motivation, emotions) explain the difference. The course zooms out **Self → Others → Organization.** Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation; **IQ vs EQ** (roughly 20% of success is IQ); **perception is not reality**; a theory explains behaviour for everyone (goal-setting; Herzberg explains quiet quitting). The **case-cracking steps**, and the rule that "I'd quit" is never the answer.

**Session 2 — Nature, Nurture & the Equation.** Nature vs nurture → the **interactionist view** wins ("nature deals the cards, nurture plays them"). **Kurt Lewin: Behaviour = f(Person, Environment)** — hold the environment constant and people still differ because of the **P factor**. The OB vocabulary (task performance, innovative work behaviour, **OCB**, voice, **CWB/deviance**, absenteeism, turnover); **attitude vs behaviour** (hidden vs visible). The **Michael Peterson** case: technical skill without people skill makes you ineffective.

**Session 3 — Ten Percent Visible.** The **behaviour iceberg** — 10% visible behaviour on 90% invisible P-factors. **Behaviour = Nature + Nurture + Memes.** **VABs** (Values, Assumptions, Beliefs, Expectations): **core** (never changes → change the team) vs **peripheral** (shiftable → you can influence). The **ABC model** of attitudes (Cognitive-Affective-Behavioural). **EVLN** — Exit · Voice · Loyalty · Neglect on **active/passive × constructive/destructive** (Hirschman).

**Session 4 — The Phone Call.** The full case method; **influence without authority** (you can't confront a peer — use **consultation** and **inspirational appeal**). Micromanagement is a negative style. The **Amelia Rogers at Tassani** case: same disease as Peterson — technically strong, weak people skills; the fix is **empathy + political astuteness.**

**Session 5 — Four Letters (MBTI).** Personality is an **enduring** set of traits (stable, slow to change). **MBTI** = four preference pairs — **E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P** → **16 types** shared by everyone in varying degree. Self-assessment traps (**SOAP**: Social-desirability, Overconfidence, Availability, Priming). **Blind spots**; validate with **360°**. MBTI's limits: **binary** (no ambiverts) and **only positive** traits.

**Session 6 — Five Castaways (OCEAN).** **Big Five = Five Factor Model = OCEAN**, five traits × six sub-traits; everyone has all five, and the **dominant = your defining trait.** Desirable levels: **O moderate, C high, E moderate, A moderate, N low.** **Maturity principle** (we trend more conscientious/stable/agreeable with age). **Trait activation** (situation switches a trait on) vs **situation strength** (strong situation overrides traits).

**Session 7 — The Dark Triad.** **Machiavellianism** (manipulation, "the end justifies the means"), **Narcissism** ("I, me, myself"), **Psychopathy** (remorseless harm). The nesting rule: **all psychopaths are Machiavellian and narcissistic, but not the reverse.** **Emergence ≠ effectiveness** — dark-trait people rise on charisma, then fall. Workplace traits (core self-evaluation, locus of control, self-monitoring, proactive personality); the humble narcissist.

**Session 8 — If You Have a Brain, You Have Biases.** Chain: **memes → VABs → preconceived notions → biases.** **Attribution theory**; **Fundamental Attribution Error** (judge others: internal over external) vs **Self-Serving Bias** (judge self: success internal, failure external); **overconfidence** (80–90% of people). The catalogue: selective perception, confirmation, **halo/horn**, contrast, stereotyping, anchoring, availability, hindsight, escalation of commitment. You can't delete biases, only **manage** them (awareness → feedback → sensitivity training/DEI).

**Session 9 — The Man Who Said No to a Raise (motivation, content).** Intrinsic vs extrinsic vs **amotivation**; carrot-and-stick (**transactional**) fails. **Herzberg** (motivators create satisfaction; **hygiene** only prevents dissatisfaction → quiet quitting); **Maslow** (1943 pyramid); **McClelland** acquired needs (**achievement, affiliation, power**); **Self-Determination Theory** (**Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness**). The **crowding-out effect** (over-paying kills intrinsic drive).

**Session 10 — Two Teams, One Roof (motivation, process).** **Goal-Setting** (SMART, Locke and Latham); **Equity** (input:output fairness); **Expectancy** (Effort → Performance → Reward; any link at zero = zero); **Self-Efficacy** (four sources: mastery, vicarious, verbal persuasion, emotional state); **Reinforcement** (positive adds, negative removes). The **Motivation Toolkit**, and the capstone **B = f(P, E)** — environment decides whether motivation becomes performance (**Crutchfield**).

**Session 11 — The Snake Under the Table (emotions & EQ).** **Discrete Emotions Theory** — six universal emotions (**anger, happiness, surprise, disgust, sadness, fear**; 2 positive, 4 negative → **negativity bias**). Each emotion has a job. **EQ beats IQ**: IQ is fixed (your height), EQ is trainable (a muscle), and high-EQ/average-IQ people outperform the highest-IQ roughly 70% of the time. **Goleman's five components** (self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation / empathy, social skill). **Empathy vs sympathy**; **emotional labor**; **emotional contagion**; **Affective Events Theory.**

### The group (Sessions 12–18)

**Session 12 — Everyone's Competent, Team Still Fails.** **Competence is necessary but not sufficient.** The **Four C's**: Competence, Capabilities (**Belbin** — thinkers/doers/supporters), Compatibility (**FIRO-B** — inclusion/control/affection, expressed vs wanted, the **CP effect**), Cohesion (**Lencioni** — trust first). **Team synergy**; the **Desert Survival** exercise (the class got **negative synergy**); groupthink, anchoring, availability.

**Session 13 — Smart People, Failing Teams.** Three instruments, one per C: **Belbin** (9 roles in 3 families — thought/action/people), **FIRO** (1952 US Navy; productivity = competence + compatibility; six-cell model; scores 0–9), **Five Dysfunctions** (vulnerability-based trust → psychological safety). **Collective intelligence beats individual intelligence** — unless **groupthink** pulls the best member back.

**Session 14 — My Way, Your Way, or a Better Way.** Conflict = disagreement; **functional vs dysfunctional**; **task / relationship / process** (relationship conflict is **always dysfunctional**). **Thomas–Kilmann** five styles on **assertiveness × cooperativeness**: competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating — **no style is best; it's contextual.** Personality predicts your style. **Negotiation**: distributive (fixed pie, win–lose) vs integrative (grow the pie, win–win). **Third parties**: mediator (no power), arbitrator (decides), conciliator (repairs). The **TKC Consulting** case — the protagonist is the **decision-maker (Mukharji)**, not the victim.

**Session 15 — Politics Is the Water.** **Power = capacity to influence.** **French and Raven's five bases**: formal (legitimate, reward, coercive) + personal (expert, referent). Effectiveness: **referent best → expert → legitimate/reward → coercive worst.** **Organizational politics** = self-serving at others' expense; **political skill** (positive, not manipulation) has **four dimensions**: social astuteness, interpersonal influence, networking ability, **apparent sincerity** (the one the Dark Triad lacks). The **Caroline Reges / Excel** case: right on the engineering, sank for lack of political skill.

**Session 16 — The Star Who Couldn't Lead.** **Executive coaching = unblock potential, not fix problems** — proof of behavioural theory. Steps: goal → assessment/self-awareness → **360°** → developmental areas → action plan → evaluation. The **Birkman Method** (usual behaviour, motivation, needs, stress behaviour) and stand-ins (Big Five, Career Anchors, Strengths Profile). The three practices — **Relational** (can be faked), **Independent** (change from within), **Self-generation** (sustained alone) — are the test for **authentic vs superficial** change. The **Coaching McKenna Lane** case; protagonist = her boss, **John Mills**.

**Session 17 — Born or Made?** Leadership is a **trainable skill.** Three theories: **trait** (born — outdated), **behavioural** (made — balance task + people), **contingency** (it depends). The **Managerial Grid** (Team style, high-high, wins). Big Five link: **extraversion → emergence, conscientiousness → effectiveness.** The **Full Range Model**: laissez-faire → transactional → **transformational** (the **Four I's**; warmth + competence). **Lewin's** autocratic/democratic/laissez-faire; **Situational Leadership**; **responsible leadership** (authentic/ethical/servant); **abusive supervision**. The big lesson: **carry a toolkit, switch by situation.**

**Session 18 — The Trolley and the Fat Man (ethics).** **Ethics = external rules; morality = your own principles.** An **ethical dilemma** = both choices are wrong. Three frameworks: **Utilitarianism** (consequences — greatest good for the greatest number; blind to individual rights), **Deontology** (the action/duty — don't kill/steal/lie, whatever the result), **Virtue Ethics** (the doer's character — "what kind of person am I becoming?"). The trolley flips because our focus shifts from **outcome** to **act**. *Deontology vs virtue: same right act, external rule vs internal character.*

### The capstone (Sessions 19–20) — *lighter, mostly project presentations*

**Sessions 19 & 20 — The Big Five project.** Not new theory — the course's "know yourself" promise cashed out. The **two-stage / two-source method** (peer interview + self-test) and **triangulation** ("never rely on a single data point"). **Perception is not reality** → the self-vs-peer gap is where **blind spots** live; **loud traits** get spotted, **quiet traits** get missed. Traits are **enduring**, but **behaviour is workable** (identify desirable workplace traits → compare → close the gap). Session 19 also delivers the professor's one-line masterclass on the **contrast effect**; Session 20 delivers the exam and grading guidance. *(These two nights were largely student presentations, so they are lighter on fresh frameworks.)*

## Part 4 — Frameworks You Can Draw From Memory

### Behaviour = f(Person, Environment) — the spine (Lewin)

```
   BEHAVIOUR  =  f ( PERSON  x  ENVIRONMENT )
   (visible)        P-factors     culture, climate,
                    personality   leadership, politics,
                    VABs, biases, justice, HR practices
                    motivation,
                    emotions
   Same environment + different person  =  different behaviour
```

### The behaviour iceberg

```
      ~10%  |  BEHAVIOUR (all you can see)
   ---- water line -------------------------------
      ~90%  |  P-FACTORS: VABs, attitudes, personality,
            |  perceptions/biases, motivation, emotions
```

### MBTI — four pairs to one four-letter code

```
   E / I   Energy:      outer world / inner world
   S / N   Information: facts / big-picture possibilities
   T / F   Decisions:   head (logic) / heart (empathy)
   J / P   Lifestyle:   structured / flexible
   =   16 types, shared by everyone in varying degree
   Limits: binary (no ambiverts); only positive traits
```

### OCEAN — the five traits and the level orgs want

```
   O  Openness ........... moderate (creative, still practical)
   C  Conscientiousness .. HIGH     (reliable, disciplined)
   E  Extraversion ....... moderate (ambivert; job-specific)
   A  Agreeableness ...... moderate (empathy, not exploited)
   N  Neuroticism ........ LOW      (calm, secure, confident)
   Only two extremes wanted: C high, N low. All but N are "desirable".
```

### The Dark Triad — nesting

```
   Machiavellianism (manipulate) ---+
                                     +===  overlap = PSYCHOPATHY
   Narcissism (self-importance) -----+   (remorseless harm)
   Rule: all psychopaths are Mac + narcissistic, NOT the reverse.
   Emergence (rise on charisma)  does NOT equal  Effectiveness (last on ethics).
```

### EVLN — four responses to dissatisfaction

```
                 CONSTRUCTIVE        DESTRUCTIVE
   ACTIVE    |   VOICE (speak up)  |  EXIT (leave)
   PASSIVE   |   LOYALTY (wait)    |  NEGLECT (bare minimum)
   Most people start at VOICE; ignored, they slide to the others.
```

### Attribution — the two signature errors

```
   Judging OTHERS  =  Fundamental Attribution Error
                     (over-weight internal, ignore situation)
   Judging SELF    =  Self-Serving Bias
                     (success = my effort; failure = circumstances)
```

### Motivation — content vs process

```
   CONTENT (what drives)          PROCESS (how it flows)
   - Maslow (hierarchy)           - Goal-Setting (SMART)
   - Herzberg (motivator/hygiene) - Equity (fairness ratio)
   - McClelland (Ach/Aff/Power)   - Expectancy (Effort,Perform,Reward)
   - Self-Determination (ACR)     - Self-Efficacy (4 sources)
                                   - Reinforcement (+/-)
```

### Expectancy chain (Vroom)

```
   EFFORT  ==(Expectancy)==  PERFORMANCE  ==(Instrumentality)==  REWARD
                                          (Valence = do I want it?)
   Any ONE link at zero  =  zero motivation.
```

### Maslow's hierarchy (bottom to top)

```
   5  Self-actualization  (full potential)
   4  Esteem              (recognition, respect)
   3  Belongingness       (friendship, inclusion)
   2  Safety              (security, health)
   1  Physiological       ("roti, kapra, makan")
```

### Goleman's five components of EI

```
   SELF   : 1 Self-awareness  2 Self-regulation  3 Motivation
   OTHERS : 4 Empathy (the core)  5 Social skill
   Chain: know, then control, then drive yourself, then feel, then connect
```

### The Four C's of a team (and their tests)

```
   Competence    =  the baseline: skills to do the work
   Capabilities  =  BELBIN   (thinkers / doers / supporters)
   Compatibility =  FIRO-B   (inclusion / control / affection)
   Cohesion      =  LENCIONI (trust-based commitment)
```

### FIRO — compatibility = Wanted meets Expressed

```
   Need: INCLUSION (belong) / CONTROL (power) / AFFECTION (closeness)
   Each has two sides:  EXPRESSED (you give)  and  WANTED (you crave)
   Fit = A's high Expressed Control  meets  B's high Wanted Control.
   Scores 0-9; no good/bad score.
```

### Lencioni — the Five Dysfunctions (base up)

```
   5  Inattention to RESULTS        (personal goals win)
   4  Avoidance of ACCOUNTABILITY
   3  Lack of COMMITMENT
   2  Fear of CONFLICT              (artificial harmony)
   1  Absence of TRUST  ==  fix this base (vulnerability-based trust)
```

### Thomas–Kilmann — five conflict styles

```
            COOPERATIVENESS (concern for others)
            low ---------------------- high
   assert | AVOIDING              ACCOMMODATING   (low assertiveness)
   iveness| (lose/lose)           (I lose, you win)
   (self) | COMPETING             COLLABORATING    (high assertiveness)
          | (I win, you lose)     (I win, you win)
                 COMPROMISING (middle: I bend, you bend)
   No style is best: match it to the situation.
```

### Negotiation — split the pie vs grow the pie

```
   DISTRIBUTIVE : fixed pie, win-lose, uses COMPETING (salary, haggling)
   INTEGRATIVE  : grow the pie, win-win, uses COLLABORATING (M&A, partnership)
```

### French and Raven — five bases of power

```
   FORMAL (from the chair)    PERSONAL (from you)
   - Legitimate (position)    - Expert (knowledge)
   - Reward                   - Referent (admiration/charisma)
   - Coercive
   Effectiveness: Referent, then Expert, then Legitimate/Reward, then Coercive (worst)
```

### Political skill — four dimensions (Ferris)

```
   1 Social astuteness   (read the room, sense motives)
   2 Interpersonal influence (persuade with a style, not force)
   3 Networking ability  (a 360-degree web of allies)
   4 Apparent sincerity  (genuine, "walk the talk")
   Drop sincerity and you look manipulative. Dark Triad lacks #4.
```

### Leadership — three theories, and the Full Range

```
   Trait       = born        (outdated)
   Behavioural = made        (balance task + people, the Managerial Grid)
   Contingency = it depends  (match style to situation + followers)

   FULL RANGE:  Laissez-faire  ==  Transactional  ==  Transformational
                (hands-off)        (carrot/stick)      (the Four I's)
   Four I's: Idealized influence, Inspirational motivation,
             Intellectual stimulation, Individualized consideration
   Emergence = extraversion ;  Effectiveness = conscientiousness
```

### Ethics — three lenses: Maths, Rulebook, Mirror

```
   UTILITARIANISM : focus on CONSEQUENCES (greatest good for greatest number)
                    blind spot: individual rights & justice
   DEONTOLOGY     : focus on the ACTION/DUTY (don't kill/steal/lie)
   VIRTUE ETHICS  : focus on the doer's CHARACTER ("who am I becoming?")
   Trolley v1 (divert) = utilitarian ; v2 (push the man) refused = deontology/virtue
```

## Part 5 — The Cases: What to Say If Asked

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
Every major IGD case turns on the **same pattern**: a **technically brilliant protagonist held back by weak people skills** — **Michael Peterson, Amelia Rogers, Caroline Reges, McKenna Lane.** For each case be ready with: (1) the core problem, (2) the framework the professor applied, (3) the resolution/insight. And remember two rules: **"I'd quit" is never the answer** (downsizing/recession), and the **protagonist is the person who must decide**, not the victim.
:::

### Michael Peterson (Session 2)
- **Problem:** at Confederation Kitchen (downsizing), a rumour may put Peterson back under a boss he clashed with; should he voice his concern to new manager William Peak?
- **Frameworks:** Behaviour = f(P, E); SWOT; perception is not reality; the case method.
- **Insight:** he's conscientious and technically strong but **blunt and tactless** — the real fix is **people skills**, not a smarter argument. Technical skill gets you hired; people skills keep you growing.

### Amelia Rogers at Tassani Communications (Session 4)
- **Problem:** creative director David Burns phoned the client directly, bypassing account director Rogers; what to do about Burns, the client, and whether to involve boss Jim Paglia.
- **Frameworks:** **influence without authority** (consultation, inspirational appeal); micromanagement; the plan **Burns → client → boss**.
- **Insight:** same disease as Peterson — she micromanaged and couldn't influence a **same-hierarchy** peer. The missing skills are **empathy (EQ)** and **political astuteness.**

### Jensen Shoe Company — Jane Kravitz & Lyndon Brooks (Sessions 7–8)
- **Problem:** a two-sided case — the same stalled project seen by a manager and her subordinate as two different realities.
- **Frameworks:** **attribution** (Jane's FAE; Lyndon's self-serving bias), **overconfidence**, **halo effect then racial/gender stereotyping**, selective perception.
- **Insight:** **perceptual distortion, not a lack of talent, broke the relationship.** Biases can't be deleted, only managed with awareness and feedback.

### Crutchfield Chemical Engineering — Lumen vs Absorb (Session 10)
- **Problem:** two near-identical R&D teams under one director diverge sharply — one thrives (Lumen), one withers (Absorb).
- **Frameworks:** every motivation theory mapped to the two teams; the diary study (Lumen about 5/7, Absorb about 3.3/7); **B = f(P, E).**
- **Insight:** the gap came from **leadership style and environment, not individual skill** — motivation only becomes performance when the environment lets it. Fix: coach/empower the controlling leader (Chip Wendel), restore the team's purpose.

### TKC Consulting (Session 14)
- **Problem:** a dual-reporting feud between Ralo and Nayak crushes top performer Aman, caught in the middle.
- **Frameworks:** conflict type (**relationship + process**, dysfunctional); Thomas–Kilmann; **third-party roles**; the nine resolution techniques.
- **Insight:** **Aman is the victim; the protagonist is chairman Mukharji**, who must decide. Fix the dual-reporting structure, restore Aman's role, use a third party plus long-term attitudinal change.

### Caroline Reges at Excel Systems (Session 15)
- **Problem:** after the Excel–Gemini merger, the CEO pushes to outsource manufacturing — which would erase VP Caroline Reges's role; she never sees the board-level power game behind it.
- **Frameworks:** the **four dimensions of political skill** (she was strong only on **apparent sincerity**); French and Raven power; LMX.
- **Insight:** she was **right about the engineering and still lost** — technical correctness needs **political skill.** Contrast with **Ria**, who read the politics and won.

### Coaching McKenna Lane (Session 16)
- **Problem:** a star performer, passed over for promotion for weak interpersonal skills, is given a year of executive coaching; her boss John Mills must decide if the change is **real or cosmetic.**
- **Frameworks:** coaching = **unblock potential**; Birkman; the **three practices** (Relational / Independent / Self-generation); the three pillars (self-awareness, collaboration, authenticity).
- **Insight:** **authentic change = inner change (Independent) that is self-sustained (Self-generation) over time** — relational gestures alone can be faked. Promote, but with conditions and follow-through.

### Supporting mini-cases (motivation)
- **Retika** (Herzberg: hygiene present, motivators absent → fix the work, not the perks).
- **Vistora** — Aditya/Mira/Karan (McClelland: achievement / affiliation / power).

## Part 6 — The Professor's Recurring Emphases

Things she returned to again and again — highest signal for the exam:

1. **One-size-fits-all fails with humans** — always start from the **P-factors.**
2. **Behaviour = f(Person, Environment)** — the single equation that ties the whole course together.
3. **Technical skill is not enough** — every case protagonist proves it; pair the technical read with a **people-skills** recommendation.
4. **Perception is not reality** — we act on bias-laden perceptions; **manage** biases, don't pretend to delete them.
5. **"If you have a brain, you have biases"** — and most self-assessment scores are inflated by **social desirability / superiority bias.**
6. **You can't change personality, but you can manage it** (and train **skills** like EQ and political skill).
7. **EQ outranks IQ** for how far you rise; **empathy can't be faked.**
8. **Trust is the centre of every team**; **competence alone is not enough.**
9. **No single leadership (or conflict) style is best** — it's contingent; carry a toolkit.
10. **The protagonist is the decision-maker, and "I'd quit" is never the answer.**

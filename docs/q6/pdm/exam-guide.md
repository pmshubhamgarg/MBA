## Part 1 — The Exam at a Glance

### Evaluation Breakdown

| Component | Weightage | Format |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Class Participation (CP)** | 20% | Includes attendance (attendance is a major part) |
| **Case Analysis (group)** | 20% | 5 cases; best 2 scores counted per group |
| **Quiz (individual)** | 20% | Announced, MCQ, 20 questions × 1 mark |
| **End-Term Examination** | 40% | Closed-book, descriptive (long answer) |

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
The 40-mark end-term is **closed-book, closed-notes, descriptive** and is **proctored via Mettl** (which tracks eyeball movement). It is **not** a new case — but it **references the five cases done in class**. Every question is **compulsory**, and each has a **word limit**. Revise concepts *and* how each concept was applied to its case.
:::

## Part 3 — Session-by-Session Rapid Recall

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
The eight highest-yield frameworks across the whole course, in one place:

- **Problem Space vs Solution Space** (S1) — understand before you build
- **RICE prioritization** (S2) — (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
- **Customer Perceived Value = Total Benefit − Total Cost** (S2)
- **Positioning six-line template** (S3) — the Geoffrey Moore promise
- **Iteration vs Pivot** (S4) — same customer served better vs a new customer entirely
- **Stage-Gate model, 8 stages** (S5) — idea → commercialization
- **A/B testing & difference-in-differences** (S6) — cause and effect
- **Rogers' Diffusion curve + the Chasm** (S9) — and the Product Life Cycle
:::

### Session 1: The Conductor Without a Baton
- **PM's three jobs**: define the product, manage development/launch/improvement, represent the customer inside the company
- **Responsibility without authority** — the paint shop story; PM as orchestra conductor; "we don't take credit, so long as it happens"
- **Problem Space vs Solution Space** — finish understanding the problem before building
- **MRD → PRD** — the translation gap between market needs and build spec
- **PM types**: digital, data, UX, product marketing, technical
- **PM vs Program Manager vs Product Owner** (Unilever soap analogy)
- **Product Life Cycle** — the PM wears a different hat at each stage
- **CCCA method** — Conceptual Clarity → Contextual Applicability
- **Likely exam angles:** define problem vs solution space; why a PM is a "conductor"; MRD vs PRD

### Session 2: From Data to Delight
- **Funnel analysis** — find where users drop off
- **RICE framework** — (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
- **B2C Elements of Value pyramid** (functional → emotional → life-changing → social impact)
- **B2B value elements** (table stakes → functional → ease of doing business → individual → inspirational)
- **Customer Perceived Value (CPV) = Total Customer Benefit − Total Customer Cost**
- **Hypothesis testing, Agile Scrum, A/B testing** (intro)
- **redBus case** — improving conversion
- **Likely exam angles:** compute/rank features with RICE; explain the value pyramid; define CPV

### Session 3: Positioning and Value Proposition
- **Positioning** — how you want the customer to perceive the product
- **Five differentiation dimensions** (product, services, channel, people, image)
- **Moore's six-line positioning template**: For [target] who [need], the [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [competitor], our product [differentiator].
- **Buyer Utility Map** — utility levers × six stages of the buyer experience cycle
- **Zenatix case** — discovering market fit
- **Likely exam angles:** write a positioning statement for a given product; fill a Buyer Utility Map

### Session 4: The Product That Pivoted Three Times
- **Product-Market Fit** — "fit the product into the market," not the other way around
- **Iteration vs Pivot** — iterate = serve the *same* customer better; pivot = change *who* the customer is (needs a new value proposition)
- **Zenatix / WattMan** — three pivots (industrial → commercial buildings → retail chains), Schneider Electric acquisition
- **Approver risk** — the adoption landmine when facilities staff feel threatened
- **Likely exam angles:** given a scenario, is it an iteration or a pivot? Why? What changes?

### Session 5: The Eight Gates (New Product Development)
- **Six categories of new products** (new-to-world, new product lines, additions, improvements, repositioning, cost reductions) — the professor stressed these are **categories, NOT strategies**
- **Stage-Gate model (8 stages)**: idea generation → idea screening → concept development → marketing strategy → business analysis → product development → market testing → commercialization
- **Idea vs Concept vs Image** — an idea is what, a concept is the elaborated version for a segment, an image is how the customer perceives it
- **Drop error vs Go error** in screening
- **Conjoint analysis** — trade-off preference measurement
- **Agile/Scrum & Extreme Programming** enter at product development
- **72% of new products fail** — hence the pipeline discipline
- **Likely exam angles:** list and explain the Stage-Gate stages; drop vs go error; idea/concept/image distinction

### Session 6: Letting Data Decide
- **Experimental research & cause-and-effect** — the six experimental designs
- **Difference-in-differences** — the placebo/blood-pressure example
- **A/B testing** = before-after-with-control
- **LendenClub P2P case** — lender/borrower personas; concept-card testing (**InstaMoney vs Kwik Money**) via **paired t-test**; prototype A/B across five journey stages
- **Feature grid**: Antes / Drivers / Fool's Gold / Neutral
- **Likely exam angles:** design an A/B test for a given feature; interpret a difference-in-differences result; classify features into the grid

### Session 7: Taking It to Market
- **GTM vs marketing plan** — GTM is the launch motion; the marketing plan is ongoing
- **Economies of scope** (Unilever/Colgate pricing)
- **Dropbox case** — freemium engine, pricing tiers, viral referral loop
- **SaaS model** — provider and user pros/cons
- **Value-based pricing**
- **Inbound vs Outbound distribution**
- **Likely exam angles:** when do you need a GTM strategy; explain freemium; SaaS pros/cons

### Session 8: Farming vs. Hunting
- **Farming (inbound) vs Hunting (outbound)** sales motions
- **LTV / CAC ratio** — the core SaaS decision metric (rule of thumb: LTV:CAC ≥ 3)
- **Blended churn, SaaS unit economics**
- **The decision process** — "set up a metric structure, then fine-tune with live data"
- **Allocating a $10M budget** with a metric, not a hunch
- **Likely exam angles:** compute LTV/CAC; justify a budget split between inbound and outbound

### Session 9: Crossing the Chasm
- **Adoption (individual) vs Diffusion (across a market)**
- **Product Life Cycle** — 4 stages (intro, growth, maturity, decline), 4Ps per stage, four launch strategies
- **Rogers' Diffusion of Innovation** — innovators (2.5%), early adopters (13.5%), early majority (34%), late majority (34%), laggards (16%)
- **The Chasm** (Geoffrey Moore) — the gap between early adopters and the early majority
- **ACCORD** — innovation attributes affecting adoption
- **Eager Sellers & Stony Buyers** resistance matrix
- **Technology Readiness Index (TRI)**
- **Nexent Systems** setup; IBM / Kalyani-Sharp decline stories
- **Likely exam angles:** draw and label Rogers' curve; explain the chasm and how to cross it; PLC stage strategies

### Session 10: The Product Roadmap Blues
- **Nexent Systems case** — Samantha Green's feature-list roadmap problem
- **The three E's** of a good roadmap
- **Product vision narrative** — tell the journey, not the feature list
- **Automated → Augmented AI evolution**
- **Roadmap construction** — five themes × three releases, mapped to **Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)**
- **Silo vs Integrate + impact analysis**
- **Likely exam angles:** convert a feature list into a themed roadmap; why themes beat features; JTBD mapping

## Part 4 — Frameworks You Can Draw From Memory

### Problem Space vs Solution Space
```
PROBLEM SPACE (understand)          SOLUTION SPACE (build)
customer research                    product definition (PRD)
market analysis                      development
ideation                             launch (GTM)
prioritization (RICE)                post-launch evaluation
────────── finish this first ──────────►
```

### RICE
```
Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
Higher score = build first
```

### The Stage-Gate Model (8 gates)
```
Idea Generation → Idea Screening → Concept Development → Marketing Strategy
→ Business Analysis → Product Development → Market Testing → Commercialization
(a gate/decision after each stage: drop or go)
```

### Rogers' Diffusion Curve + The Chasm
```
Innovators  Early Adopters │ CHASM │ Early Majority  Late Majority  Laggards
  2.5%          13.5%      │       │      34%             34%          16%
                        (crossing the chasm is where most products die)
```

### LTV / CAC
```
LTV = (avg revenue per customer × gross margin) / churn rate
CAC = total sales & marketing spend / new customers acquired
Healthy SaaS: LTV : CAC ≥ 3 ; payback < 12 months
```

### The Positioning Template (Geoffrey Moore)
```
For [target customer] who [statement of need],
the [product name] is a [product category]
that [key benefit / reason to buy].
Unlike [primary competitor],
our product [key differentiator].
```

## Part 5 — The Five Cases: What to Say If Asked

:::danger[Must Know — Exam Critical]
The end-term will ask you to **explain a concept and how it was applied in a case**. For each of the five cases, be ready with: (1) the core problem, (2) the framework the professor applied to it, (3) the resolution/insight. These are the highest-probability descriptive questions.
:::

### redBus (Sessions 1-2)
- **Problem:** improve conversion on India's largest bus-ticketing platform
- **Frameworks:** funnel analysis, RICE prioritization, problem vs solution space, A/B testing
- **Insight:** find where users drop off, prioritize fixes by RICE, test before rolling out

### Zenatix / WattMan (Session 4)
- **Problem:** an energy-management startup searching for product-market fit
- **Frameworks:** iteration vs pivot, JTBD, approver risk
- **Insight:** three pivots (industrial → commercial buildings → retail chains); acquired by Schneider Electric — a pivot changes *who* the customer is, and each pivot needed a new value proposition

### LendenClub (Session 6)
- **Problem:** new product development for a P2P lending platform in the digital space
- **Frameworks:** A/B testing, difference-in-differences, concept-card testing (InstaMoney vs Kwik Money), paired t-test, the Antes/Drivers/Fool's Gold/Neutral feature grid
- **Insight:** let data — not opinion — decide which concept and features win

### Dropbox (Sessions 7-8)
- **Problem:** go-to-market and budget allocation for a freemium SaaS product
- **Frameworks:** GTM vs marketing plan, freemium engine, SaaS unit economics, LTV/CAC, farming vs hunting
- **Insight:** allocate spend with a metric structure (LTV/CAC), then fine-tune with live data

### Nexent Systems (Sessions 9-10)
- **Problem:** Samantha Green inherits a roadmap that is just a feature list ("the product roadmap blues")
- **Frameworks:** product vision narrative, themes × releases, JTBD, Rogers' diffusion, the chasm, TRI
- **Insight:** a roadmap should tell a journey mapped to customer jobs, not enumerate features

## Part 6 — Professor's Recurring Emphases

Things Professor Banerjee returned to repeatedly — highest signal for the exam:

1. **"Am I in the problem space or the solution space right now?"** — the discipline of separating the two
2. **"We don't take credit, so long as it happens"** — influence without authority is the PM's real skill
3. **"Iterate with patience"** — there is no sure-shot formula; resilience and iteration are the job
4. **"There are always trade-offs"** — every PM decision is a hard choice (Parle-G shrinkflation)
5. **Categories are not strategies** — he corrected this explicitly for the six new-product categories
6. **Let data decide** — A/B testing and experiments over opinion (LendenClub)
7. **A metric, not a hunch** — budget and GTM decisions run on LTV/CAC (Dropbox)
8. **Themes, not features** — a roadmap tells a journey mapped to jobs to be done (Nexent)
